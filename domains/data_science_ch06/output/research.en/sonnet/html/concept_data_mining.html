Data Mining

Definition. Let $\mathcal{D} = \{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\} \subset \mathcal{X}$ be a finite sample drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution $P$ over a measurable space $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{F})$. Data mining is the algorithmic extraction of statistically significant, previously unknown, and actionable patterns from $\mathcal{D}$. Formally, a pattern is a statement $\varphi$ in some language $\mathcal{L}$ such that (i) $\varphi$ holds on a non-negligible fraction of $\mathcal{D}$, quantified by a support threshold $\sigma \in (0,1]$, and (ii) $\varphi$ would not be expected to hold under a null model $P_0$ at significance level $\alpha$. The tension between (i) and (ii) is not cosmetic: it defines the core statistical challenge of the field.

Theorem (Bonferroni bottleneck). Let $|\mathcal{L}|$ be the cardinality of the pattern language. If each pattern $\varphi_i$ is tested independently at level $\alpha$ under $P_0$, the expected number of false positives is $|\mathcal{L}| \cdot \alpha$. For association-rule mining with binary itemsets over $d$ items, $|\mathcal{L}| = 3^d - 2^{d+1} + 1$, which grows exponentially in $d$.

Proof sketch. By linearity of expectation and the union bound, $\mathbb{E}[\text{false positives}] = \sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{L}|} \Pr[\text{reject } H_0^{(i)} \mid H_0^{(i)} \text{ true}] = |\mathcal{L}| \cdot \alpha$. The itemset count follows from inclusion-exclusion on the power set $2^{[d]}$, subtracting singletons and the empty set. $\square$

This observation underpins every modern correction strategy: Bonferroni control of the family-wise error rate (FWER), Benjamini–Hochberg control of the false discovery rate (FDR), and the more recent pattern-team approaches that treat the selected set as a random variable and bound its risk directly (Hämäläinen, 2012; Webb, 2007).

Literature context. The Apriori algorithm (Agrawal & Srikant, 1994) established the antimonotonicity of support as a pruning device, reducing exponential search to tractable lattice traversal. FP-Growth (Han et al., 2000) eliminated repeated database scans via a compressed tree structure, achieving near-linear scaling in practice. However, neither addresses statistical validity: a pattern with support $\sigma$ on $\mathcal{D}$ may not generalize to $P$. The statistically valid pattern mining (SVPM) programme (Terada et al., 2013; Llinares-López et al., 2015) reframes mining as a multiple-testing problem under a Westfall–Young permutation framework, enabling FWER control without sacrificing power by restricting the family to testable patterns — those whose support falls above the minimum testable threshold given $n$ and $\alpha$.

An open question concerns the power–interpretability frontier: deep representation-learning methods (autoencoders, transformer embeddings) identify latent patterns that classical itemset miners miss, but these patterns resist the propositional form that SVPM requires. Bridging distributional representations with statistically grounded significance tests remains unsolved.
