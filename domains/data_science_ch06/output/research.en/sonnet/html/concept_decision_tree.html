Decision Tree

Definition. A decision tree is a recursive partitioning of the input space $\mathcal{X} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ into axis-aligned rectangular regions, each assigned a constant prediction. Formally, a binary tree $T$ of depth $D$ defines a partition $\{R_1, \ldots, R_m\}$ of $\mathcal{X}$ such that the predictor takes the form

$$f_T(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} c_j \,\mathbf{1}[\mathbf{x} \in R_j],$$

where $c_j \in \mathbb{R}$ (regression) or $c_j \in \{0,1\}^K$ (classification). Each internal node selects a coordinate $k \in [d]$ and a threshold $\tau$, routing left if $x_k \leq \tau$ and right otherwise. The tree structure and the split parameters $(k, \tau)$ at every node are jointly the object of learning.

The Greedy Splitting Criterion and Its Optimality Gap. The canonical training algorithm (CART; Breiman et al. 1984) greedily minimizes an impurity measure at each node. For regression, this is the weighted mean-squared error reduction:

$$\Delta_\text{MSE}(k,\tau) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2 \;-\; \frac{n_L}{n}\sigma^2_L(k,\tau) \;-\; \frac{n_R}{n}\sigma^2_R(k,\tau),$$

where $n_L, n_R$ are the counts in the left and right child and $\sigma^2_{L/R}$ their response variances. For classification the Gini impurity $G = \sum_k \hat{p}_k(1-\hat{p}_k)$ or information gain replaces the MSE term.

Theorem (CART approximation ratio, informal; Klusowski 2021). For a regression tree of depth $D$ learned on $n$ samples from a Lipschitz target $f^*$, the excess risk satisfies

$$\mathbb{E}[\|f_T - f^*\|^2] = O\!\left(n^{-\frac{2}{d+2}} \log n\right)$$

under standard regularity, matching minimax rates for piecewise-constant estimators up to logarithmic factors. This rate degrades exponentially with dimension $d$—the manifestation of the curse of dimensionality for decision trees—but the constant depends only weakly on the global Lipschitz constant.

What Is Known and What Remains Open. The CART greedy criterion is not globally optimal: finding the depth-$D$ tree minimizing training loss exactly is NP-hard (Hyafil & Rivest 1976). This gap has motivated a line of work on optimal decision trees (Bertsimas & Dunn 2017; Aglin et al. 2020, the GOSDT framework) that formulate tree learning as integer programming and prove tighter generalization bounds for the resulting trees. A current open question is whether the computational intractability can be circumvented under distributional assumptions (e.g., margin conditions, low intrinsic dimension). Separately, the interpolation regime—where fully-grown trees memorize training data—exhibits benign overfitting only under strong structural constraints, unlike neural networks or random forests, making single-tree generalization in the over-parameterized regime an active area.

Measure: (i) training loss gap between greedy and optimal solutions, (ii) test accuracy, (iii) tree size.
