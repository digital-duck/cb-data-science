Bootstrap Aggregating

Imagine your class is guessing how many jellybeans fill a giant jar. You ask one friend — maybe she guesses 200, but she only glanced at the top. Then you ask another friend — he peeks from the side and says 350. A third friend squints at the bottom and says 280.

No single friend sees the whole picture. But here is the magic: when you collect all their guesses and find the middle value, something wonderful happens. The mistakes start cancelling each other out. Together, the group is smarter than any one person alone.

Bootstrap aggregating works exactly like this. A computer trains not one model, but dozens of them — each one learning from a different random scoop of the same data, like each friend getting a different peek at the jar. Some scoops repeat information; that is fine. Then, when a new question arrives, every model votes, and the most popular answer wins.

The trick is that random differences in the scoops mean each model makes different mistakes. When you average across many different mistakes, most errors wash away, leaving only the truth.

Now you try: Poll five friends on how many steps it takes to walk to the library. Average their guesses. How close is the group to the real answer compared to any single friend?
