Binary Multiclass Classification

The Sorting Game

Imagine you work at the post office on Animal Island. Every morning, a big bag of letters arrives. Your job: put each letter in the right mailbox.

At first, the island only had two animals — a cat and a dog. Every letter went into one of two boxes. Easy! That's called binary classification — choosing between exactly two things. "Is it this, or that?"

But then more animals moved in: a parrot, a fish, a rabbit, and a tortoise. Now you have many mailboxes. Each letter still goes into exactly one box — but now you're picking from a whole crowd. That's multiclass classification — choosing from three or more possibilities.

The clever part? The rules you use are the same. You look at clues on the letter — the handwriting, the smell, the little paw print — and match them to the right animal. More animals just means more clues to learn, more boxes to check.

In machine learning, binary is "yes or no," and multiclass is "which one?"
