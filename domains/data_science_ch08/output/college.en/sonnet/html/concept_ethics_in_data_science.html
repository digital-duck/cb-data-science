Ethics In Data Science

Definition. Ethics in data science is the systematic study of moral obligations that arise when collecting, processing, analyzing, and deploying data-driven models. It addresses three interlocking domains: privacy (the right of individuals to control information about themselves), fairness (the requirement that algorithmic decisions do not unjustly discriminate), and accountability (the obligation to explain, audit, and correct automated systems). Formally, a data pipeline $\mathcal{P}: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ is said to satisfy demographic parity with respect to a protected attribute $A \in \{0,1\}$ if

$$P(\hat{Y} = 1 \mid A = 0) = P(\hat{Y} = 1 \mid A = 1),$$

where $\hat{Y}$ denotes the model's binary prediction. Violations of this condition constitute a measurable, quantifiable form of algorithmic bias.

Worked Example. A credit-scoring model trained on historical loan data produces acceptance rates of $78\%$ for group $A=0$ and $52\%$ for group $A=1$. The disparity ratio is $52/78 \approx 0.667$, below the widely-cited four-fifths rule threshold of $0.8$. One corrective strategy is reweighting: assign each training sample $(x_i, y_i)$ a weight

$$w_i = \frac{P(A)}{P(A = a_i)},$$

effectively rebalancing the empirical distribution so the model cannot exploit group membership as a proxy feature. After reweighting and retraining, practitioners re-evaluate both the disparity ratio and overall accuracy, accepting a possible accuracy–fairness trade-off as an explicit design decision rather than an overlooked artifact.

Key Theorem (Impossibility of Simultaneous Fairness Criteria). Chouldechova (2017) proved that when base rates differ across groups ($P(Y=1 \mid A=0) \neq P(Y=1 \mid A=1)$), no classifier can simultaneously satisfy demographic parity, equalized odds, and calibration. This result is not a failure of engineering; it is a mathematical fact that forces practitioners to choose which fairness criterion the application's social context demands, and to document that choice transparently.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, Rational, simplify

p0, p1 = Rational(78, 100), Rational(52, 100)
disparity_ratio = simplify(p1 / p0)
four_fifths_satisfied = disparity_ratio >= Rational(4, 5)

print(f"Disparity ratio: {disparity_ratio}")          # 2/3
print(f"Four-fifths rule satisfied: {four_fifths_satisfied}")  # False

Running this cell makes the bias quantification reproducible and auditable — a habit that operationalizes the accountability principle from the outset of any project.
