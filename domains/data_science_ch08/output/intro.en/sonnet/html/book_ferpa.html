Concept Book: Ferpa

Payoff

Picture a high-school junior named Maya who discovers, by accident, that her disciplinary record contains a mistake — a suspension that never happened. She wants to see the file, correct it, and make sure no college admissions office ever reads the error. A generation ago, she would have had no path forward. The record belonged to the school, full stop.

FERPA — the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act — changes that story at its root. It says: an education record is about you, therefore you have a stake in it. This feels right before it is ever formalised, because it matches our intuition about ownership of information. If a document describes your life, you should be able to read it, challenge it, and control who else does.

The formalisation is surprisingly clean. FERPA defines three interlocking rights for any eligible student (or their parent, while the student is a minor):

$$\text{Rights} = \{\,\text{Access},\;\text{Amendment},\;\text{Consent}\,\}$$

Access means the institution must show you your records within 45 days of request. Amendment gives you a formal channel to contest inaccurate entries. Consent means the school cannot release your records to a third party — an employer, another university, a government agency — without your written permission, with narrowly defined exceptions.

What makes FERPA the natural capstone of this book is that it converts an abstract value (educational privacy) into a decision procedure: at every release point, ask whether consent exists or a named exception applies. That is the same logic we have practised throughout — turning a principle into a rule you can actually run.

Now you try. Suppose a professor emails your final grade to your parent without asking you first. Walk through the three FERPA rights: which one is implicated, what remedy exists, and who holds the burden of proof? Once you can answer that, pick one of the statutory exceptions — "school officials with legitimate educational interest" is a good starting place — and explore where its boundary lies.
