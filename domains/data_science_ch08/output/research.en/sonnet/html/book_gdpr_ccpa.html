Payoff

Definition. Let $\mathcal{D}$ denote a dataset of $n$ records over a universe $\mathcal{U}$, and let $\mathcal{M} : \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{R}$ be a randomized mechanism releasing a response in range $\mathcal{R}$. A regulatory regime $\mathfrak{R}$ (instantiated here by GDPR Article 5 / CCPA § 1798.100) imposes a tuple of constraints $\langle \Pi_{\mathrm{purpose}}, \Pi_{\mathrm{minimization}}, \Pi_{\mathrm{retention}}, \Pi_{\mathrm{subject}} \rangle$, where each $\Pi_{\cdot}$ is a predicate on $(\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{D}, t)$ indexed by processing time $t$. Compliance is the conjunction $\bigwedge_i \Pi_i = \top$.

Theorem (Necessity of Composition Accounting). If $\mathcal{M}$ is the sequential composition of $k$ sub-mechanisms $\mathcal{M}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{M}_k$, each $(\varepsilon_i, \delta_i)$-differentially private, then the composed mechanism satisfies $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-differential privacy with $\varepsilon = \min\!\left(\sum_i \varepsilon_i,\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\right)$ under advanced composition (Dwork et al. 2010). Regulatory minimization ($\Pi_{\mathrm{minimization}}$) is therefore not merely a legal obligation but a direct bound-tightening operation: reducing $k$ or restricting query sensitivity $\Delta f$ shrinks $\varepsilon$ monotonically, making legal compliance and privacy-loss accounting formally equivalent pressures.

Proof sketch. The advanced composition theorem gives $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}} = O\!\left(\sqrt{k \ln(1/\delta)}\cdot\varepsilon_0\right)$ for homogeneous $\varepsilon_i = \varepsilon_0$. Minimization reduces $k$; purpose limitation constrains the support of allowable $\mathcal{M}_i$; together they enforce a tighter budget. $\square$

Literature context. The alignment between differential privacy and GDPR's data-minimization principle is formalised in Domingo-Ferrer et al. (2021, ACM CSUR) and extended to CCPA's opt-out semantics by Nissim & Wood (2018). Open questions include: (i) no universally accepted mapping from $\varepsilon$ to a legal "appropriate technical measure"; (ii) tension between GDPR's right-to-erasure and the immutability requirements of distributed ledgers; (iii) cross-jurisdictional composition when GDPR and CCPA apply simultaneously to the same pipeline—formal convexity of the joint feasible set is unproven.

Suggested investigation. Implement a synthetic-data pipeline under $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP and instrument it with a compliance oracle that checks each $\Pi_i$ at query time. Vary $k$ and $\Delta f$ while measuring the empirical privacy loss via a membership-inference adversary (Shokri et al. 2017). Plot the Pareto frontier of utility loss versus $\varepsilon$ and annotate it with the regulatory threshold implied by Article 25 GDPR ("privacy by design"). Does the frontier shift when CCPA's sale-restriction predicate is added as an additional constraint?

Report-writing note. A write-up of this investigation should: state the DP guarantee with explicit $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ values and composition theorem invoked; justify the choice of sensitivity $\Delta f$ by reference to the data schema; report membership-inference AUC as the empirical privacy metric; and map each $\Pi_i$ to a numbered regulatory article so a legal reader can audit the correspondence. Conclude with the gap between the smallest $\varepsilon$ achieved and any sector-specific threshold found in recent regulatory guidance—this gap is the open research question your work begins to close.
