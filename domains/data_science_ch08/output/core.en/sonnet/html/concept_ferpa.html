Ferpa

Definition

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), enacted by the United States Congress in 1974 (20 U.S.C. § 1232g), is a federal statute that governs the privacy of education records held by institutions receiving federal funding. An education record is defined as any record, file, document, or other material that (i) contains information directly identifying a student and (ii) is maintained by an educational agency or institution, or by a party acting on its behalf. FERPA confers two primary rights upon eligible students (those 18 years of age or older, or enrolled in postsecondary education): the right to inspect and review their own education records within 45 days of a formal request, and the right to request amendment of records believed to be inaccurate or misleading. Disclosure of personally identifiable information (PII) from education records to third parties is prohibited without the student's written consent, subject to enumerated exceptions—most notably the school officials exception (legitimate educational interest), the health or safety emergency exception, and the directory information exception, the last of which permits disclosure of non-sensitive data such as name, major, and enrollment status provided the institution has given adequate public notice and the student has not opted out.

Worked Example

A university registrar receives a request from a parent seeking the grade report of their 20-year-old child enrolled full-time. Because the student is over 18 and enrolled postsecondary, FERPA rights belong solely to the student. The registrar must decline unless (a) the student has submitted a signed FERPA release form authorizing parental access, or (b) the institution can demonstrate the student is a dependent for federal tax purposes under 26 U.S.C. § 152. Absent either condition, releasing the transcript constitutes a FERPA violation that can jeopardize the institution's federal funding.

Key Principle

FERPA Consent Requirement. Let $R$ be an education record and $T$ a third party external to the institution. Disclosure $D(R, T)$ is lawful if and only if

$$D(R, T) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{Consent}(S) \;\lor\; \exists\, e \in \mathcal{E} : e \text{ applies},$$

where $S$ denotes the eligible student and $\mathcal{E}$ is the finite set of statutory exceptions enumerated under 34 C.F.R. § 99.31.

from sympy import FiniteSet, Piecewise, symbols, Eq, simplify

# Model the FERPA disclosure decision symbolically
consent, exception = symbols('consent exception', boolean=True)

# Lawful disclosure iff consent OR a valid exception applies
disclosure_lawful = consent | exception

cases = [
    (consent, exception, disclosure_lawful),
    (True,  False, True),
    (False, True,  True),
    (False, False, False),
]

print(f"{'Consent':<10} {'Exception':<12} {'Lawful?'}")
print("-" * 35)
for c, e, result in cases:
    print(f"{str(c):<10} {str(e):<12} {result}")
