Percentiles

Definition. Let $X$ be a real-valued random variable with cumulative distribution function $F(x) = P(X \leq x)$. For $p \in (0, 1)$, the $p$-th quantile (or $100p$-th percentile) of $X$ is the value

$$Q(p) = \inf\{x \in \mathbb{R} : F(x) \geq p\}.$$

For a finite data set $\{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\}$ arranged in non-decreasing order $x_{(1)} \leq x_{(2)} \leq \cdots \leq x_{(n)}$, the $100p$-th percentile is commonly estimated by the order statistic $x_{(\lceil np \rceil)}$, where $\lceil \cdot \rceil$ denotes the ceiling function. Percentiles partition the distribution into hundredths: the 50th percentile is the median, while the 25th and 75th percentiles are the first and third quartiles, $Q_1$ and $Q_3$, respectively. Their difference $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$ is the interquartile range, a robust measure of spread.

Worked Example. Suppose a class of ten students earns the scores

$$55,\; 62,\; 67,\; 70,\; 74,\; 78,\; 81,\; 85,\; 91,\; 96.$$

Find the 80th percentile. Here $n = 10$ and $p = 0.80$, so the index is $\lceil 10 \times 0.80 \rceil = \lceil 8 \rceil = 8$. The 80th percentile is therefore $x_{(8)} = 85$, meaning approximately 80% of students scored at or below 85.

Theorem (Monotonicity and Equivariance). Let $Q_X(p)$ denote the $p$-th quantile of $X$. For any monotone increasing function $g$,

$$Q_{g(X)}(p) = g\!\left(Q_X(p)\right).$$

Proof sketch. Since $g$ is increasing, $g(X) \leq g(x) \iff X \leq x$, so $F_{g(X)}(g(x)) = F_X(x)$. Taking the infimum over $\{t : F_{g(X)}(t) \geq p\}$ yields the result. $\square$

This equivariance property is practically significant: percentiles of log-transformed income data, for instance, map back to income percentiles by exponentiation without recomputation.

Lab Cell.

import numpy as np
from sympy import stats, Symbol, N

# Empirical percentiles
scores = [55, 62, 67, 70, 74, 78, 81, 85, 91, 96]
p80 = np.percentile(scores, 80, method='higher')
iqr = np.percentile(scores, 75) - np.percentile(scores, 25)
print(f"80th percentile: {p80}")   # 85
print(f"IQR: {iqr}")               # 19.25

Running this cell confirms $Q(0.80) = 85$ and reveals that the middle 50% of scores spans roughly 19 points, indicating moderate dispersion around the median.
