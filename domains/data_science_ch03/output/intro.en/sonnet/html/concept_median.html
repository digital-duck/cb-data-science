Median

Imagine five friends line up by height: short, shorter-still, medium, tall, tallest. Who stands exactly in the middle? The one with two friends on each side — that's the median.

It's the value right in the center of a sorted list, not the average. Why does that matter? Suppose four kids earn $1 in allowance and one lucky kid earns $100. The average is about $20 — but nobody actually gets $20. The median is $1, which tells the true story of what most kids experience.

The key move is always the same: sort first, then find the middle.

  • Odd number of values: the middle one wins.

    • $\{3, 7, 9\}$ → median $= 7$

  • Even number of values: average the two middle ones.

    • $\{3, 7, 9, 11\}$ → median $= \dfrac{7+9}{2} = 8$

    Now you try. Five students scored $62, 95, 70, 88, 70$ on a quiz. Sort the scores from smallest to largest, then find the median. Does it feel like a "typical" score to you?
