The Lemonade Stand Mystery

Imagine you sell lemonade all summer. Every day you write down how many cups you sold. By August, you have a notebook crammed with numbers — but you can't see anything. Which day was your best? Which week was slowest? Your eyes just swim.

Here's the secret: a computer can read all those numbers in one blink and show you the story hiding inside them.

That's what Python data analysis does. Python is a friendly programming language, and with it you can load your notebook of numbers, ask questions like "what's the average?" or "show me the worst day," and get a picture back — a chart, a table, a clear answer.

Think of it like a super-powered magnifying glass. Raw numbers are just dots. Python turns those dots into a picture you can understand.

In Python, we often use a tool called pandas to hold our data in neat rows and columns — like a spreadsheet that can talk back.

Now you try: List the temperatures for five days this week. Which day was warmest? Could you figure that out faster with a helper — or by staring at the list?
