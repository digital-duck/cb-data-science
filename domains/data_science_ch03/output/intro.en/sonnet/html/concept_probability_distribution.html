Probability Distribution

Imagine you have a bag of 10 marbles: 5 red, 3 blue, and 2 yellow. Every time you reach in without looking, you pull one out, then put it back. Do this a hundred times — which color shows up most? Red, almost definitely. Yellow, barely at all.

That pattern — which outcomes show up and how often — is called a probability distribution. It's like a personality portrait of randomness. It doesn't tell you what will happen next, but it tells you what to expect over many tries.

Think of it as a scoreboard nature keeps for every possible outcome. Each outcome gets a share of 100%, and all the shares must add up to exactly $100\%$ (or $1$, in math language):

$$P(\text{red}) + P(\text{blue}) + P(\text{yellow}) = 1$$

Some distributions are lopsided (like our marble bag). Others are perfectly even, like a fair coin: heads and tails each get $\frac{1}{2}$.

Now you try: Roll a six-sided die 30 times and tally each number. Does each face show up about $\frac{1}{6}$ of the time? What does your tally chart look like — flat or bumpy?
