Quartiles

Definition. Let $X$ be a real-valued random variable with cumulative distribution function $F$. The $p$-th quantile of $F$ is the generalized inverse $Q(p) = F^{-1}(p) = \inf\{x \in \mathbb{R} : F(x) \geq p\}$ for $p \in (0,1)$. The quartiles are the three special cases $Q_1 = Q(1/4)$, $Q_2 = Q(1/2)$ (the median), and $Q_3 = Q(3/4)$. The interquartile range is $\mathrm{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$.

This clean theoretical definition conceals a persistent ambiguity: given a finite sample $x_{(1)} \leq x_{(2)} \leq \cdots \leq x_{(n)}$, no single canonical estimator of $Q(p)$ emerges from first principles. The sample quantile function must interpolate between order statistics, and the choice of interpolation scheme is genuinely consequential.

Theorem (Asymptotic normality of sample quantiles). Let $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ be i.i.d.\ from $F$ with density $f$ satisfying $f(Q(p)) > 0$. Define the sample quantile $\hat{Q}_n(p)$ via any consistent interpolation scheme. Then

$$\sqrt{n}\bigl(\hat{Q}_n(p) - Q(p)\bigr) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\, \frac{p(1-p)}{f(Q(p))^2}\right).$$

Proof sketch. Write $\hat{Q}_n(p) = F_n^{-1}(p)$ where $F_n$ is the empirical CDF. The Bahadur representation (Bahadur 1966) gives $\hat{Q}_n(p) = Q(p) + \frac{p - F_n(Q(p))}{f(Q(p))} + O(n^{-3/4}\log n)$ a.s. The leading term $\frac{p - F_n(Q(p))}{f(Q(p))}$ has mean zero and variance $\frac{p(1-p)}{f(Q(p))^2 n}$ by the variance of the empirical CDF, so the CLT applies. $\square$

The variance $p(1-p)/[f(Q(p))^2 n]$ grows as $f(Q(p)) \to 0$, quantifying the familiar intuition that sparse regions of the distribution make quantile estimation hard.

Literature context. Hyndman and Fan (1996, The American Statistician) catalogued nine distinct sample quantile definitions in common use, differing in their type of interpolation between $x_{(\lfloor np \rfloor)}$ and $x_{(\lceil np \rceil)}$. Four are implemented in NumPy and R by default, yet produce meaningfully different results on small samples. This definitional multiplicity is not merely pedagogical: it affects downstream statistics such as IQR-based outlier thresholds and boxplot whiskers. A more recent line of work (Ma and Genton 2011; Serfling and Xiao 2007) extends the IQR concept to multivariate settings via halfspace depth, where the notion of "between the 25th and 75th percentile" requires a complete rethinking of order.

Suggested investigation. Fix $n \in \{10, 30, 100\}$ and draw samples from three distributions: $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, and a heavy-tailed $t_3$. For each $(n, F)$ pair, compute $Q_1$ and $Q_3$ under all nine Hyndman–Fan definitions and record mean absolute error against the true theoretical quartile over $10^4$ Monte Carlo replications. Determine which definition minimizes bias and MSE for each distribution and sample size, and verify that the asymptotic variance formula above tightens with $n$ for the continuous-interpolation estimators (types 7–9). As a secondary experiment, compare IQR-based outlier detection rates ($x \notin [Q_1 - 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR},\, Q_3 + 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}]$) across definitions: even small definitional differences can shift flagging thresholds substantially under skewed data.
