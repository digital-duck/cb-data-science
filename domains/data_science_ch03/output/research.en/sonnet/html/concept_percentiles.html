Percentiles

Definition. Let $X$ be a real-valued random variable with cumulative distribution function $F(x) = \Pr(X \leq x)$. The $p$-th percentile (or $100p$-th quantile) of $X$ is the value

$$Q(p) = F^{-1}(p) := \inf\{x \in \mathbb{R} : F(x) \geq p\}, \quad p \in (0,1).$$

This generalized inverse (the quantile function) is well-defined for any CDF, including those with jumps or flat regions, and coincides with the ordinary inverse when $F$ is strictly increasing and continuous. The 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles are the quartiles $Q_1, Q_2, Q_3$; the interquartile range $\mathrm{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$ is a canonical robust measure of spread.

Theorem (Quantile Equivariance). For any strictly monotone increasing function $g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and random variable $X$,

$$Q_{g(X)}(p) = g(Q_X(p)).$$

Proof. By the monotone transformation law, $\Pr(g(X) \leq y) = \Pr(X \leq g^{-1}(y))$ for $g$ strictly increasing. Hence $F_{g(X)}(y) = F_X(g^{-1}(y))$, and

$$F_{g(X)}^{-1}(p) = \inf\{y : F_X(g^{-1}(y)) \geq p\} = g\!\left(\inf\{x : F_X(x) \geq p\}\right) = g(Q_X(p)). \qquad \square$$

This result is practically important: a log-transform of data preserves the rank ordering, so percentiles of $\log X$ exponentiate back to percentiles of $X$ — a fact exploited in lognormal modeling throughout reliability engineering and finance.

Estimation. Given an i.i.d. sample $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ with order statistics $X_{(1)} \leq \cdots \leq X_{(n)}$, the empirical quantile $\hat{Q}(p) = X_{(\lceil np \rceil)}$ is strongly consistent by the Glivenko–Cantelli theorem. Under mild regularity ($f = F'$ exists and $f(Q(p)) > 0$), the asymptotic distribution is

$$\sqrt{n}\bigl(\hat{Q}(p) - Q(p)\bigr) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\, \frac{p(1-p)}{f(Q(p))^2}\right),$$

a classical result derived via the delta method applied to the empirical CDF (see Serfling, Approximation Theorems of Mathematical Statistics, 1980, §2.3). The variance blows up near the tails ($p \to 0$ or $1$), which is why extreme quantile estimation remains an active research area connected to extreme value theory (EVT) and the Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem.

What is open. Quantile estimation under dependence, heavy tails, and high dimensions is far from settled. Conformal prediction reframes quantile estimation as a coverage guarantee problem (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023), while quantile regression (Koenker & Bassett, 1978) extends the concept to conditional quantiles $Q_{Y|X}(p)$ via the asymmetric loss $\rho_p(u) = u(p - \mathbf{1}_{u < 0})$, whose minimizer is exactly $Q(p)$ — a characterization that motivates neural quantile networks and conformal risk control.
