Trend Cycle Component

Definition. Let $\{y_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$ be a time series observed at equally spaced intervals. The trend-cycle component, denoted $\tau_t$, is the smooth, slowly-varying constituent of $y_t$ that captures long-run movements and medium-frequency oscillations jointly, abstracting away seasonal fluctuations and irregular noise. Under the additive decomposition model introduced in the previous section,

$$y_t = \tau_t + s_t + \varepsilon_t,$$

where $s_t$ is the (already-defined) seasonal component and $\varepsilon_t$ is white noise. The trend-cycle is accordingly defined residually as $\tau_t = y_t - s_t - \varepsilon_t$, and is estimated in practice by applying a symmetric moving-average filter that suppresses high-frequency variation.

Worked Example. Consider quarterly GDP growth rates (in percent) over eight periods:

$$\mathbf{y} = (2.1,\ 2.8,\ 1.9,\ 2.5,\ 3.1,\ 3.7,\ 3.0,\ 3.6).$$

Applying a $2\times 4$ moving average — the standard filter for quarterly data with $m=4$ — yields

$$\hat{\tau}_t = \frac{1}{8}\!\left(y_{t-2} + 2y_{t-1} + 2y_t + 2y_{t+1} + y_{t+2}\right), \quad t = 3, \ldots, 6.$$

For $t=3$: $\hat{\tau}_3 = \tfrac{1}{8}(2.1 + 2(2.8) + 2(1.9) + 2(2.5) + 3.1) = \tfrac{1}{8}(20.6) = 2.575$. The resulting sequence $\{\hat{\tau}_t\}$ reveals a gentle upward trend overlaid with mild cyclical curvature, obscured in the raw series by quarterly seasonality.

Key Theorem (Low-Pass Extraction). If $\{y_t\}$ is covariance-stationary and a symmetric linear filter $\hat{\tau}_t = \sum_{k=-K}^{K} a_k\, y_{t-k}$ with $\sum_k a_k = 1$ is applied, then the spectral density of $\hat{\tau}_t$ satisfies

$$S_{\hat{\tau}}(\omega) = \left|\mathcal{A}(e^{i\omega})\right|^2 S_y(\omega),$$

where $\mathcal{A}(e^{i\omega}) = \sum_k a_k e^{-ik\omega}$ is the filter's frequency-response function. Because the $2\times 4$ weights concentrate $|\mathcal{A}|^2$ near $\omega = 0$, the filter acts as a low-pass gate: seasonal and noise frequencies are attenuated, leaving $\hat{\tau}_t$ as a clean extract of the trend-cycle already implicit in $y_t$.

Lab Cell (SymPy/statsmodels).

import pandas as pd
from statsmodels.tsa.seasonal import seasonal_decompose

y = pd.Series([2.1, 2.8, 1.9, 2.5, 3.1, 3.7, 3.0, 3.6])
result = seasonal_decompose(y, model='additive', period=4, extrapolate_trend='freq')
print(result.trend)   # τ_t estimates
