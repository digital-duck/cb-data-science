What Naive Forecasting Achieves

Throughout this text we have built the machinery of time-series analysis piece by piece: stationarity, autocorrelation, moving averages, and error decomposition. The concept of naive forecasting brings that machinery into sharp focus by providing its indispensable baseline. A naive forecast for a univariate series $\{y_t\}$ at horizon $h$ is defined by

$$\hat{y}_{t+h \mid t} = y_t,$$

that is, the best prediction of any future value is simply the most recently observed value. For seasonal series with period $s$, the seasonal naive variant reads

$$\hat{y}_{t+h \mid t} = y_{t + h - s\lceil h/s \rceil},$$

which repeats the corresponding observation from the most recent complete cycle.

The deceptive simplicity of these formulae is precisely their power. Every sophisticated model — ARIMA, exponential smoothing, gradient-boosted trees, neural sequence models — must justify its added complexity by beating this benchmark on held-out data. If it cannot, Occam's razor demands the simpler answer.

Worked Example

Consider monthly retail electricity demand (MWh) recorded over three years. Splitting the series at month 25 and forecasting months 26–36, the seasonal naive model reproduces the values from months 14–24. Computing mean absolute scaled error

$$\text{MASE} = \frac{\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}|y_{t+h} - \hat{y}_{t+h\mid t}|}{\frac{1}{T-s}\sum_{t=s+1}^{T}|y_t - y_{t-s}|}$$

yields $\text{MASE} = 1$ by construction for the naive model, since the denominator is the in-sample one-step naive error. Any model achieving $\text{MASE} < 1$ is demonstrably superior; any model exceeding 1 has failed even this minimal standard. This single number converts raw forecast errors into an interpretable, scale-free verdict.

Key Theorem

Theorem (Optimality under random walk). If $y_t = y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t$ where $\varepsilon_t \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} (0, \sigma^2)$, then $\hat{y}_{t+h\mid t} = y_t$ minimises mean squared forecast error for all $h \geq 1$, and no measurable function of the history $\mathcal{F}_t$ can improve upon it.

Proof sketch. Under the random walk, $y_{t+h} = y_t + \sum_{k=1}^{h}\varepsilon_{t+k}$. The innovations $\varepsilon_{t+k}$ are independent of $\mathcal{F}_t$, so $\mathbb{E}[y_{t+h}\mid\mathcal{F}_t] = y_t$. The conditional expectation uniquely minimises MSE among all $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable predictors. $\blacksquare$

Lab Cell

import sympy as sp

# Demonstrate MASE formula symbolically
T, s, H = sp.symbols('T s H', positive=True, integer=True)
naive_denom = sp.Sum(sp.Abs(sp.Symbol('y_t') - sp.Symbol('y_{t-s}')),
                     (sp.Symbol('t'), s + 1, T)) / (T - s)
sp.pprint(naive_denom)

Invitation

Naive forecasting is not the end of the story — it is the door through which every serious forecasting application must pass. Its role as a universal benchmark makes it the natural culmination of the concepts developed here: you cannot know whether a model is good until you know whether it beats doing nothing. The most immediate arena in which this benchmark has reshaped practice is demand planning and inventory management, where seasonal naive models regularly outperform enterprise software costing orders of magnitude more.
