Concept Book: Augmented Dickey Fuller Test

Data Science Ch05 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

You have spent this chapter learning to ask a deceptively simple question: is this sequence wandering, or is it anchored? The Augmented Dickey-Fuller test is the tool that answers that question with mathematical precision instead of guesswork.

Here is what it actually does. Given a time series $\{y_t\}$, the ADF test fits a regression of the form

$$\Delta y_t = \alpha + \beta t + \gamma y_{t-1} + \sum_{k=1}^{p} \delta_k \Delta y_{t-k} + \varepsilon_t$$

and then checks whether $\gamma$ is significantly negative. If $\gamma = 0$, the series has a unit root — it wanders without returning home, the way a random walk does. If $\gamma < 0$, the series is stationary — it has a mean it keeps coming back to. The extra terms $\Delta y_{t-k}$ are the "augmented" part; they mop up any lingering pattern so the test stays honest.

Why does this matter? Because nearly every serious technique for forecasting — from stock-price models to climate projections to economic growth equations — assumes you know which kind of series you are working with. Feeding a wandering series into a method designed for an anchored one produces nonsense, even if the math looks clean.

The ADF test is the gateway check. It tells you whether you can move forward safely or whether you first need to transform your data (for instance, by taking differences) to make it stationary.

Practice problem. A student records the daily high temperature in their city for 30 days and computes first differences $\Delta y_t = y_t - y_{t-1}$. They run an ADF test on the original series and get a $p$-value of $0.41$, then run it again on the differenced series and get $p = 0.02$. What do these results tell them about the original series, and is the differenced series a better candidate for further modeling? Explain your reasoning in two or three sentences.
