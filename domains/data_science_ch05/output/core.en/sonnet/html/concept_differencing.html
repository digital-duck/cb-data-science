Differencing

You already have a feel for this: if a friend ran 5 km yesterday and 8 km today, the change is 3 km. Differencing is just that idea applied systematically to a whole sequence of numbers — subtract each value from the one that follows it.

The structure. Given a sequence $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_n$, the first difference sequence is:

$$\Delta a_k = a_{k+1} - a_k$$

So the first difference sequence has $n - 1$ terms. You can then difference again to get second differences, $\Delta^2 a_k = \Delta a_{k+1} - \Delta a_k$, and so on.

Why it matters. Differencing reveals hidden patterns. Consider the squares:

$$1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25, \ldots$$

First differences: $3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9, \ldots$ — an arithmetic sequence! Second differences: $2,\ 2,\ 2, \ldots$ — constant.

Whenever the $d$-th differences are all the same nonzero number, the original sequence is described by a degree-$d$ polynomial. This is a powerful shortcut: you don't need to guess the formula; the differences tell you the degree automatically.

Simple rule. If the $d$-th difference row is constant, the sequence is polynomial of degree $d$. If no difference row ever becomes constant, the sequence grows faster than any polynomial (exponential, for instance).

Practice problem. Consider the sequence $2,\ 6,\ 12,\ 20,\ 30$.

  • Compute the first and second differences.
  • What degree polynomial do you expect describes this sequence?
  • Can you guess a formula for the $n$-th term? (Hint: factor each term.)
