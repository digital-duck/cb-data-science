Cyclic Component

Definition

A cyclic component of a time series is a quasi-periodic fluctuation whose wavelength exceeds one full seasonal cycle but is not monotone. Formally, let a discrete time series $\{y_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$ admit the additive decomposition

$$y_t = T_t + C_t + S_t + \varepsilon_t,$$

where $T_t$ is the trend, $S_t$ the seasonal component (period $m$ fixed and known), and $\varepsilon_t$ zero-mean noise. The cyclic component $C_t$ is the residual signal obtained after removing trend and seasonality; it satisfies $\mathbb{E}[C_t] = 0$ and has a dominant pseudo-period $P$ with $P \gg m$. Unlike $S_t$, the period $P$ is neither fixed nor required to be an integer multiple of the sampling interval.

Worked Example

Suppose monthly GDP growth (in percent) over 120 months is modelled as

$$y_t = \underbrace{0.02\,t}_{T_t} + \underbrace{1.5\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi t}{12}\right)}_{S_t} + \underbrace{3\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi t}{48}\right)}_{C_t} + \varepsilon_t.$$

Here $S_t$ captures a one-year seasonal swing ($m = 12$), while $C_t$ represents a four-year business cycle ($P = 48$ months). After fitting a linear trend and subtracting the seasonal mean for each calendar month, the residual series $\hat{C}_t = y_t - \hat{T}_t - \hat{S}_t$ retains the four-year oscillation. A periodogram of $\hat{C}_t$ would show a spectral peak near frequency $f = 1/48$ cycles per month, confirming the isolation of the cyclic signal.

Key Theorem

Spectral Separation Theorem (informal). If $T_t$, $C_t$, $S_t$, and $\varepsilon_t$ have non-overlapping spectral support — that is, their power spectra are concentrated at disjoint frequency bands — then each component is identifiable from $y_t$ up to estimation error of order $O(T^{-1/2})$. In practice, overlapping spectra require band-pass filters (e.g., the Hodrick–Prescott or Baxter–King filter) to extract $C_t$ with controlled leakage.

Lab Cell (SymPy)

import sympy as sp

t = sp.Symbol('t', positive=True)

T_t = 2 * t / 100
S_t = 3 * sp.cos(2 * sp.pi * t / 12) / 2
C_t = 3 * sp.cos(2 * sp.pi * t / 48)

y_t = T_t + S_t + C_t

# sp.simplify (new this section): reduces an expression to its canonical form
residual = sp.simplify(y_t - T_t - S_t)
print("Cyclic component:", residual)     # expects 3*cos(pi*t/24)

omega = sp.pi / 24                       # angular frequency in residual
P = sp.simplify(2 * sp.pi / omega)
print("Pseudo-period P =", P, "months") # expects 48

Running this cell confirms that symbolic subtraction of the trend and seasonal terms recovers $C_t$ exactly, illustrating the additive structure underlying empirical decomposition methods.
