Trend

Definition. Let $\{y_t\}_{t=1}^T$ be a discrete-time stochastic process. Its trend component $\tau_t$ is the low-frequency, slowly-varying systematic movement obtained via the additive decomposition $y_t = \tau_t + s_t + \varepsilon_t$, where $s_t$ denotes seasonality and $\varepsilon_t$ is a zero-mean irregular component. The critical distinction is whether $\tau_t$ is deterministic — $\tau_t = \alpha + \beta t$ for fixed $\alpha, \beta$ — or stochastic, governed by $\tau_t = \tau_{t-1} + \eta_t$ with $\eta_t \overset{iid}{\sim} (0, \sigma_\eta^2)$ (a random walk, hence $I(1)$). These regimes are not aesthetically equivalent: conflating them yields spurious regression, a pathology rigorously established by Phillips (1986) showing that OLS $t$-statistics diverge when both $y_t$ and $x_t$ are independent $I(1)$ processes.

Theorem (Beveridge–Nelson Decomposition). Any $I(1)$ process with Wold representation $\Delta y_t = C(L)\varepsilon_t$, $C(1) \neq 0$, admits a unique decomposition into a random walk and a stationary remainder: $y_t = C(1)\sum_{s=1}^t \varepsilon_s + \tilde{y}_t$, where $\tilde{y}_t$ is $I(0)$. The permanent component $C(1)\sum \varepsilon_s$ is the stochastic trend; no signal-extraction oracle separates it cleanly from the cycle. The Hodrick–Prescott filter instead solves

$$ \min_{\{\tau_t\}} \sum_{t=1}^T (y_t - \tau_t)^2 + \lambda \sum_{t=2}^{T-1}(\Delta^2 \tau_{t+1})^2, $$

trading fidelity against smoothness via hyperparameter $\lambda$. Hamilton (2018, ReStat) shows the HP filter introduces spurious periodicity when applied to unit-root data — an unresolved methodological controversy.

What is known; what is open. The Augmented Dickey–Fuller and KPSS tests offer complementary null hypotheses (unit root vs. trend-stationarity) but have low power in small samples and near-unit-root DGPs. Structural-break-robust tests (Perron 1989, Zivot–Andrews 1992) remain active research: consistent break-point selection with trend estimation is not settled when the number of breaks is unknown.

Suggested investigation. Generate paired series — one trend-stationary ($y_t = 0.1t + \varepsilon_t$, $\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$), one random-walk ($y_t = y_{t-1} + \eta_t$) — for $T \in \{50, 200, 1000\}$. Apply ADF, KPSS, and HP extraction at $\lambda \in \{6.25, 100, 1600\}$. Record rejection rates across 500 Monte Carlo replications. Extend by injecting a structural break at $t = T/2$ and assessing whether the Zivot–Andrews procedure recovers the break date.
