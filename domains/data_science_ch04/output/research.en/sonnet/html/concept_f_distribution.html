F Distribution

Definition. Let $U \sim \chi^2(d_1)$ and $V \sim \chi^2(d_2)$ be independent chi-squared random variables with $d_1$ and $d_2$ degrees of freedom, respectively. The random variable

$$F = \frac{U/d_1}{V/d_2}$$

is said to follow an F distribution with parameters $d_1$ and $d_2$, written $F \sim F(d_1, d_2)$. Its probability density function on $(0, \infty)$ is

$$f(x; d_1, d_2) = \frac{1}{B(d_1/2,\, d_2/2)} \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^{d_1/2} \frac{x^{d_1/2 - 1}}{\left(1 + \frac{d_1}{d_2}x\right)^{(d_1+d_2)/2}},$$

where $B(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the beta function. The mean exists for $d_2 > 2$ and equals $d_2/(d_2-2)$; the variance exists for $d_2 > 4$.

Theorem (Density derivation via ratio transformation). If $U$ and $V$ are independent with $U \sim \chi^2(d_1)$ and $V \sim \chi^2(d_2)$, then $F = (U/d_1)/(V/d_2)$ has the density above.

Proof sketch. Write the joint density of $(U, V)$ as a product of gamma densities with shape parameters $d_1/2$ and $d_2/2$. Apply the change of variables $F = (U/d_1)/(V/d_2)$, $W = V$, with Jacobian $\partial(U,V)/\partial(F,W) = d_1 W / d_2$. Integrate out $W$ using the identity $\int_0^\infty w^{a-1} e^{-w(1 + d_1 F/d_2)/2}\,dw = 2^a \Gamma(a)(1 + d_1 F/d_2)^{-a}$ for $a = (d_1+d_2)/2$. The remaining constant assembles into $B(d_1/2, d_2/2)^{-1}$ via the standard beta–gamma relation $B(a,b) = \Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)$. $\square$

A related identity of practical importance: $F(d_1, d_2) \stackrel{d}{=} 1/F(d_2, d_1)$, which implies $F_{1-\alpha}(d_1, d_2) = 1/F_\alpha(d_2, d_1)$, reducing the number of tabled critical values required.

Literature context. The F distribution was introduced by R.A. Fisher (1924) and named in his honor by Snedecor (1934); Lehmann and Romano's Testing Statistical Hypotheses (3rd ed., 2005) provides a rigorous treatment of the optimality of F-tests under normality. What remains less settled is the behavior under model misspecification: the classical ANOVA F-test is known to be robust to mild non-normality (Box, 1953), yet the precise breakdown point under heavy tails or heteroscedasticity remains an active area—see Wilcox (2012, Introduction to Robust Estimation and Hypothesis Testing) for a practical account and Romano & Wolf (2005, JASA) for permutation-based corrections that recalibrate the test without assuming $F(d_1, d_2)$ null distributions.

Suggested investigation. A productive graduate project is a simulation study of F-test size and power under departure from normality. Generate samples from $t_\nu$, $\text{Laplace}$, and skewed distributions; apply the standard two-sample variance-ratio test and one-way ANOVA F-test; record empirical Type I error rates against nominal $\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10\}$ across a grid of sample sizes $n \in \{10, 30, 100\}$. Compare against Bartlett's test and Levene's test as competitors. A natural extension is to implement Welch's correction and verify that the approximate $F(d_1', d_2')$ distribution with non-integer degrees of freedom—derived via the Welch–Satterthwaite equation—recovers nominal size where the classical test fails.
