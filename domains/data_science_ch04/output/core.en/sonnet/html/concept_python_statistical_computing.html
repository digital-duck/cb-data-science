Python Statistical Computing

You've probably calculated a mean or median by hand — now Python lets you apply those same ideas to thousands of data points in milliseconds. The key shift is thinking of data as a collection you transform, not a list of numbers you process one at a time.

From formula to code

Recall the mean of $n$ values:

$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$$

In plain Python that is sum(data) / len(data). The library NumPy brings you closer to the math:

import numpy as np
data = np.array([4, 7, 13, 2, 1])
print(np.mean(data))    # 5.4
print(np.median(data))  # 4.0

NumPy arrays look like lists, but every operation works element-by-element across the whole collection at once — making them much faster on real datasets.

The three-anchor summary

For any dataset, three numbers tell most of the story:

| Statistic | Symbol | NumPy call | |---|---|---| | Mean | $\bar{x}$ | np.mean(data) | | Median | $\tilde{x}$ | np.median(data) | | Std. deviation | $\sigma$ | np.std(data) |

Standard deviation measures spread: $\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2}$. A large $\sigma$ means the values are far from the center.

The pattern to remember

When $\bar{x}$ and $\tilde{x}$ differ noticeably, your data is skewed — a few extreme values are pulling the mean away from the middle. The median is the more honest center in that case.
