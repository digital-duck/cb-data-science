Margin Of Error

Imagine you're guessing how many jellybeans are in a jar. You peek carefully and say, "I think there are about 200." But you're not exactly sure — maybe it's 195, maybe 210. So you say, "I think it's 200, give or take 15."

That "give or take" part? That's the margin of error.

It's your honest way of saying: my best guess is this number, but the real answer is probably somewhere nearby. It's not an admission of failure — it's a sign of careful thinking! A guess with no margin of error is actually suspicious, like someone claiming to count every jellybean without touching the jar.

Scientists, pollsters, and weather forecasters all use margins of error. When a news report says "60% of kids prefer pizza, $\pm 5\%$," that means the true number is probably somewhere between $55\%$ and $65\%$.

$$\text{estimate} \pm \text{margin of error} = \text{range of likely truth}$$
