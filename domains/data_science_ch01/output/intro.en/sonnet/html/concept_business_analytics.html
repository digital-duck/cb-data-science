Business Analytics

Definition. Business analytics is the systematic application of statistical methods, computational algorithms, and mathematical modeling to organizational data for the purpose of informing decisions. Formally, given a dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ where $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^p$ is a vector of predictor variables and $y_i \in \mathbb{R}$ is an outcome of interest, business analytics seeks a function $\hat{f}: \mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}$ that minimizes a loss $\mathcal{L}$ over $\mathcal{D}$. The discipline spans three horizons: descriptive analytics (summarizing historical data), predictive analytics (estimating $\hat{f}$), and prescriptive analytics (optimizing decisions given $\hat{f}$).

Worked Example. A retail firm records weekly unit sales $y$ alongside two predictors: advertising spend $x_1$ (thousands of USD) and average price $x_2$ (USD). The ordinary least-squares (OLS) estimator minimizes the residual sum of squares:

$$\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = \underset{\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^3}{\arg\min} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_{1i} - \beta_2 x_{2i}\bigr)^2.$$

Suppose the fitted model yields $\hat{y} = 120 + 3.5\,x_1 - 2.1\,x_2$. The coefficient $\hat{\beta}_1 = 3.5$ implies that each additional thousand dollars of advertising is associated with 3.5 additional units sold, ceteris paribus. A prescriptive question follows immediately: given a budget constraint $x_1 \leq B$, set $x_1 = B$ to maximize expected sales.

Key Theorem (Gauss–Markov). Under the assumptions that $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}] = \mathbf{0}$, $\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}) = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}$, and $\mathbf{X}$ has full column rank, the OLS estimator $\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{y}$ is the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE): for any other linear unbiased estimator $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}$, $\operatorname{Var}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) - \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}})$ is positive semi-definite. This guarantees minimum variance among the class of unbiased linear estimators, providing a rigorous foundation for regression-based analytics.

import sympy as sp

x1, x2, b0, b1, b2 = sp.symbols('x1 x2 b0 b1 b2')
y_hat = b0 + b1*x1 + b2*x2

# Substitute fitted coefficients
model = y_hat.subs({b0: 120, b1: sp.Rational(7, 2), b2: sp.Rational(21, 10)})
print("Fitted model:", model)

# Marginal effect of advertising
d_sales_d_x1 = sp.diff(model, x1)
print("∂ŷ/∂x1 =", d_sales_d_x1)   # Expected: 7/2
