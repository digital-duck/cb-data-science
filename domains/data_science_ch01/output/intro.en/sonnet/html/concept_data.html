Data

Imagine you and your friends each pick a favourite colour. You write them all down on sticky notes: red, blue, blue, green, red, red. That list — those sticky notes — is data.

Data is just a collection of facts about the world. It could be colours, names, temperatures, or how many jumping jacks you did each day this week. By itself, one sticky note isn't very interesting. But when you collect many of them together, something magical happens: you can start to see patterns. "Hey, most of us picked red!"

Think of data as raw ingredients in a kitchen. Flour, eggs, and sugar sitting in bowls don't tell you much. But once you look at them together and start asking questions — How much of each do I have? Which one do I have the most of? — you're already doing something powerful.

We use a simple idea to organise data: each piece of information is called an observation, and each type of information (like "colour" or "count") is called a variable.

Now you try: Ask five friends how many pets they have. Write down each answer. You just collected your first dataset!
