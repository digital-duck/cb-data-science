Data Preparation

The Messy Backpack Problem

Imagine your teacher asks everyone to bring their favorite snack to share. Kids arrive with chips still in crinkled bags, apples with stickers on them, and cookies crumbled at the bottom of a lunchbox. Before anyone can eat, someone has to sort, clean, and arrange everything neatly on a tray.

That's exactly what data preparation is.

A computer is like that teacher — it can only work well with information that is clean, organized, and in the right shape. Raw data is messy: numbers written differently, missing pieces, or things mixed up that shouldn't be. Preparing data means washing away the mess so the computer can actually learn something useful.

Think of it as three kitchen steps: clean (remove the dirt), sort (put like things together), and measure (make sure everything is the right size). If you skip any step, the recipe goes wrong.

Formally, we say data preparation transforms a raw dataset $D$ into a ready dataset $D'$ where every entry is complete, consistent, and comparable.
