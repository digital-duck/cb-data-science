Scatterplot Color Encoding

Definition. A scatterplot represents a bivariate dataset $\{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ by mapping each observation to a point in the Cartesian plane. Color encoding extends this to a third variable $z_i$ by assigning each point a color determined by a function $c : Z \to \mathcal{C}$, where $Z \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ (or a finite label set) and $\mathcal{C}$ is a color space such as RGB or HSL. The complete visual mapping is therefore the triple $(x_i, y_i, z_i) \mapsto (\text{position}, \text{hue/lightness})$, encoding three dimensions of information in two-dimensional ink.

Color scales fall into three families: sequential (monotone lightness, for ordered $z$), diverging (two hues meeting at a neutral midpoint, for $z$ centered on a reference value), and qualitative (maximally distinct hues, for categorical $z$). Choosing incorrectly — for instance, applying a qualitative palette to a continuous variable — destroys the visual ordering that the data possess.

Worked Example. Consider the Palmer Penguins dataset with variables: bill length $x$ (mm), flipper length $y$ (mm), and species $z \in \{\text{Adelie}, \text{Chinstrap}, \text{Gentoo}\}$. Because species is nominal, a qualitative palette is correct. Plotting reveals that Gentoo penguins occupy a distinct region of high $y$, low $x$, immediately visible through color separation even before any statistical test is performed.

Key Theorem (Perceptual Discriminability). Under the CIE $L^a^b^$ model, the perceived difference between two colors is $\Delta E = \sqrt{(\Delta L^)^2 + (\Delta a^)^2 + (\Delta b^)^2}$. Pairs with $\Delta E < 2.3$ are indistinguishable to the average observer (just-noticeable difference). A well-designed qualitative palette guarantees $\Delta E \geq 10$ between any two assigned colors, ensuring reliable category discrimination independent of $z$-value proximity.

Lab Cell.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from palmerpenguins import load_penguins

df = load_penguins().dropna()
palette = {"Adelie": "#E69F00", "Chinstrap": "#56B4E9", "Gentoo": "#009E73"}

fig, ax = plt.subplots()
for species, group in df.groupby("species"):
    ax.scatter(group["bill_length_mm"], group["flipper_length_mm"],
               label=species, color=palette[species], alpha=0.7)
ax.set_xlabel("Bill Length (mm)")
ax.set_ylabel("Flipper Length (mm)")
ax.legend(title="Species")
plt.show()

Experiment by replacing the qualitative palette with a sequential colormap (viridis) and observe how categorical structure becomes illegible, reinforcing the theorem's practical import.
