Concept Book: Pareto Chart

Data Science Ch09 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A Pareto chart is a composite statistical display that ranks categorical factors by their contribution to a cumulative total, overlaying a bar chart of individual frequencies with a line graph of the running cumulative percentage. Formally, let $\{c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_k\}$ be $k$ categories with associated non-negative magnitudes $\{f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_k\}$. After sorting so that $f_{(1)} \geq f_{(2)} \geq \cdots \geq f_{(k)}$, the cumulative share at rank $j$ is

$$C_j = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{j} f_{(i)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} f_{(i)}} \times 100\%.$$

The chart plots $f_{(j)}$ as bars and $C_j$ as a connected curve on a secondary axis. This construction makes the Pareto principle — the empirical observation that roughly 80% of effects trace to 20% of causes — immediately legible.

Worked example. A quality-control team logs 200 defects across five root causes: incorrect assembly (90), missing component (55), surface scratch (30), mislabelling (15), other (10). Sorting and computing cumulative shares gives $C_1 = 45\%$, $C_2 = 72.5\%$, $C_3 = 87.5\%$, $C_4 = 95\%$, $C_5 = 100\%$. The chart reveals that addressing the top two causes eliminates nearly three-quarters of all defects — a quantitative justification for prioritised resource allocation.

Key theorem (Lorenz–Pareto duality). The cumulative curve of a Pareto chart is a discrete Lorenz curve when magnitudes are normalised to unit sum. Consequently, the Gini coefficient $G = 1 - 2\int_0^1 L(x)\,dx$ — where $L$ is the piecewise-linear Lorenz curve — measures the degree of concentration: $G = 0$ indicates perfect uniformity, $G = 1$ maximal skew. A steep initial rise in the Pareto curve corresponds to $G$ approaching 1, confirming that the "vital few" categories dominate.

Lab cell.

import sympy as sp
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

causes  = ["Assembly", "Missing part", "Scratch", "Label", "Other"]
counts  = [90, 55, 30, 15, 10]

paired  = sorted(zip(counts, causes), reverse=True)
vals, labels = zip(*paired)
total   = sum(vals)
cumulative = [sum(vals[:i+1]) / total * 100 for i in range(len(vals))]

fig, ax1 = plt.subplots()
ax2 = ax1.twinx()
ax1.bar(labels, vals, color="steelblue")
ax2.plot(labels, cumulative, color="crimson", marker="o")
ax1.set_ylabel("Frequency"); ax2.set_ylabel("Cumulative %")
plt.title("Pareto Chart — Defect Root Causes"); plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()
