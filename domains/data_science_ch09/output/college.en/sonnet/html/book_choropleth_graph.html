Concept Book: Choropleth Graph

Data Science Ch09 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A choropleth graph is a thematic map in which geographic regions — states, counties, census tracts, or any polygon-delimited unit — are filled with a color or shading proportional to a scalar variable $z : \mathcal{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ defined on the region set $\mathcal{R}$. Formally, let $(\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{T})$ be a planar graph whose vertices are regions and whose edges encode shared boundaries, and let $f : [z_{\min}, z_{\max}] \to C$ be a continuous colormap into a perceptually uniform color space. The choropleth assigns color $f(z(r))$ to every point in region $r \in \mathcal{R}$. The result is a function from geographic space to visual signal — the natural endpoint of the journey from raw tabular data through spatial join, graph construction, and scalar aggregation.

Worked example. Suppose $\mathcal{R}$ is the fifty U.S. states and $z(r)$ is the 2020 median household income. After normalizing to $[0,1]$ via $\hat{z}(r) = \bigl(z(r) - z_{\min}\bigr)/(z_{\max} - z_{\min})$, a sequential colormap $f$ maps $\hat{z} = 0$ to pale yellow and $\hat{z} = 1$ to deep blue. Maryland, with $z \approx \$94{,}000$, receives a deep blue fill; Mississippi, with $z \approx \$46{,}000$, receives a pale fill. A single glance reveals the spatial clustering of affluence along the Northeast corridor — a pattern invisible in a ranked table.

Theorem (Spatial Autocorrelation Detection). If $\mathcal{R}$ carries a spatial weights matrix $W$ with $w_{ij} = 1$ iff regions $i, j$ share a boundary, then Moran's $I$ statistic

$$I = \frac{n}{\sum_{i,j} w_{ij}} \cdot \frac{\sum_{i,j} w_{ij}(z_i - \bar{z})(z_j - \bar{z})}{\sum_i (z_i - \bar{z})^2}$$

equals $1$ under perfect positive autocorrelation and $-1/(n-1)$ under spatial randomness. A choropleth that exhibits visible color clustering is a visual hypothesis that $I \gg 0$; the statistic confirms or refutes it rigorously.

Lab cell.

import geopandas as gpd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

world = gpd.read_file(gpd.datasets.get_path("naturalearth_lowres"))
ax = world.plot(column="gdp_md_est", cmap="YlOrRd",
                legend=True, figsize=(12, 6),
                missing_kwds={"color": "lightgrey"})
ax.set_title("World GDP Estimate — Choropleth")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

Connection to GIS mapping. Geographic Information Systems treat every layer as a function over spatial coordinates. The choropleth is the GIS layer in its simplest closed form: polygon geometry paired with a scalar attribute and a rendering rule. Mastering it unlocks the full GIS pipeline — raster overlay, spatial interpolation with kriging ($\hat{z}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i \lambda_i z(\mathbf{x}_i)$ with $\sum_i \lambda_i = 1$), and multi-layer dashboards that fuse satellite imagery with census statistics.
