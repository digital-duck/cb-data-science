Three D Visualization

Three-dimensional visualization is the graphical representation of functions, surfaces, and vector fields defined over a subset of $\mathbb{R}^3$ or rendered as embedded objects in three-dimensional space. Formally, a surface is a continuous map $\mathbf{r}: D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ given by

$$\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl(x(u,v),\; y(u,v),\; z(u,v)\bigr),$$

where $D$ is the parameter domain. When $z$ is expressed explicitly as a function of two variables, the surface takes the form $z = f(x, y)$, and the graph is the set $\{(x, y, f(x,y)) \mid (x,y) \in D\}$.

Worked example. Consider the paraboloid $f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2$ over the square $D = [-2,2]\times[-2,2]$. At the origin, $f(0,0)=0$, giving the minimum. Along the circle $x^2+y^2=1$ the surface sits at height $z=1$, and at the boundary corners $(\pm2,\pm2)$ it reaches $z=8$. Plotting a grid of $(x_i, y_j, f(x_i,y_j))$ points and connecting them with mesh lines reveals a bowl-shaped surface whose cross-sections parallel to the $xy$-plane are circles and whose cross-sections containing the $z$-axis are upward-opening parabolas.

Key theorem (gradient and surface normal). Let $f: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuously differentiable. At any point $(a,b)$, the vector

$$\mathbf{n} = \left(-f_x(a,b),\; -f_y(a,b),\; 1\right)$$

is normal to the surface $z = f(x,y)$ at the point $(a, b, f(a,b))$. Consequently, the tangent plane at that point is

$$z - f(a,b) = f_x(a,b)(x - a) + f_y(a,b)(y - b).$$

This result links the visual geometry of the surface—how steeply it rises—to the analytic gradient $\nabla f = (f_x, f_y)$.

Lab cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, lambdify, cos, sqrt
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

x, y = symbols('x y')
expr = x**2 + y**2                          # paraboloid

f = lambdify((x, y), expr, 'numpy')
u = np.linspace(-2, 2, 40)
X, Y = np.meshgrid(u, u)
Z = f(X, Y)

fig = plt.figure()
ax = fig.add_subplot(111, projection='3d')
ax.plot_surface(X, Y, Z, cmap='viridis', alpha=0.85)
ax.set_xlabel('x'); ax.set_ylabel('y'); ax.set_zlabel('z')
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

Replace expr with any SymPy expression to explore arbitrary surfaces; lambdify bridges symbolic computation and fast NumPy evaluation for smooth interactive plots.
