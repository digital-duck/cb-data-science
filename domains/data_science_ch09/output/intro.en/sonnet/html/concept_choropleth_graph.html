Choropleth Graph

Picture this: You and your friends are playing a game where you color a map of your school building. Rooms that are really hot get colored dark red, cool rooms get light blue, and rooms in between get other shades. Now anyone can glance at the map and instantly know which rooms need better air conditioning — no reading lists of numbers required!

The big idea: A choropleth graph is exactly that — a map where regions are shaded with different colors (or darkness) to show how much of something exists there. Darker usually means more, lighter means less.

Why it works: Our eyes are brilliant at spotting patterns in color. Comparing numbers in a table is slow and tiring. But spotting "the whole south side is darker" takes half a second.

Geographers use choropleth maps to show rainfall across countries. Doctors use them to track where illnesses spread. Scientists use them to show population or pollution.

The key rule: Every region gets exactly one color, based on one measurement. The color is the data.
