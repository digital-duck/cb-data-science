Payoff

Imagine you are lost in a giant grocery store, looking for the cereal aisle. Now imagine someone hands you a map — suddenly everything makes sense. You can see where you are, where you want to go, and the fastest way to get there. That feeling of the world clicking into place? That is exactly what GIS mapping gives us, but for the whole planet.

GIS — Geographic Information Systems — lets us layer questions on top of maps. Where do floods happen most? Which neighborhoods have no parks? Where should the new hospital go? Every question becomes a picture you can actually see and touch.

All the ideas you have built — coordinates, scale, patterns, direction — now work together. A location is just a point $( x, y )$. A route is a path connecting points. A pattern is what you notice when thousands of points appear on the same map at once.

Maps do not just show the world. They help us fix it.

Now you try: Pick one place in your town — a park, a school, a flood-prone street — and draw the one layer of information you wish decision-makers could see. What story would your map tell?
