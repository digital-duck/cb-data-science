Correlation Heatmap

A correlation heatmap is a matrix visualization in which each cell $(i, j)$ encodes the Pearson correlation coefficient between variables $X_i$ and $X_j$ through a colour scale, allowing rapid identification of linear relationships across many variable pairs simultaneously.

Definition. Given a data matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ with columns $X_1, \ldots, X_p$, the sample correlation matrix is $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times p}$ with entries

$$r_{ij} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_{ki} - \bar{x}_i)(x_{kj} - \bar{x}_j)}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_{ki}-\bar{x}_i)^2}\,\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_{kj}-\bar{x}_j)^2}}.$$

Each entry satisfies $-1 \le r_{ij} \le 1$, with $r_{ii} = 1$ for all $i$. The heatmap renders $\mathbf{R}$ as a colour-coded grid, typically mapping $-1$ to a cool hue (e.g., blue) and $+1$ to a warm hue (e.g., red), with white or neutral tones at $0$.

Worked Example. Suppose we measure three variables — study hours $X_1$, sleep hours $X_2$, and exam score $X_3$ — across $n = 5$ students:

| Student | $X_1$ | $X_2$ | $X_3$ | |---------|--------|--------|--------| | 1 | 2 | 8 | 55 | | 2 | 4 | 7 | 65 | | 3 | 6 | 6 | 75 | | 4 | 8 | 5 | 85 | | 5 | 10 | 4 | 95 |

Computing pairwise correlations yields $r_{12} \approx -1.0$, $r_{13} \approx +1.0$, and $r_{23} \approx -1.0$. The resulting heatmap displays a deep-red cell at $(1,3)$ and deep-blue cells at $(1,2)$ and $(2,3)$, instantly communicating that study time and score move together while sleep hours oppose both.

Key Theorem (Symmetry and Positive Semi-Definiteness). The correlation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ is symmetric ($\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{R}^\top$) and positive semi-definite; that is, $\mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{R}\, \mathbf{v} \ge 0$ for all $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^p$. Consequently, all eigenvalues of $\mathbf{R}$ are non-negative, which underpins Principal Component Analysis: the heatmap's block structure foreshadows the groupings that PCA will extract as leading eigenvectors.

Lab Cell.

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import seaborn as sns
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from sympy import Matrix, Rational

# Symbolic correlation matrix for the worked example
R = Matrix([
    [1,     Rational(-1), 1],
    [Rational(-1), 1,    Rational(-1)],
    [1,     Rational(-1), 1]
])
print("Eigenvalues:", R.eigenvals())

# Numeric heatmap
data = pd.DataFrame({"Study": [2,4,6,8,10],
                     "Sleep": [8,7,6,5,4],
                     "Score": [55,65,75,85,95]})
corr = data.corr()
sns.heatmap(corr, annot=True, cmap="coolwarm", vmin=-1, vmax=1)
plt.title("Correlation Heatmap")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
