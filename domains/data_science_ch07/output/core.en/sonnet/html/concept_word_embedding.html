Word Embedding

You already have the intuition: words can be represented as arrows (vectors) in a high-dimensional space, and words with similar meanings end up pointing in similar directions. Now let's look at what that actually means in practice.

A word embedding assigns every word a list of numbers — its coordinates in that space. A simple example with just two dimensions might look like:

$$\text{cat} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.9 \\ 0.2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{dog} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.85 \\ 0.25 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{car} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.1 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix}$$

Real embeddings use hundreds of dimensions, but the principle is the same. Each dimension captures some hidden feature — maybe one tracks "animal-ness," another tracks "size," and so on. The model learns these features automatically from enormous amounts of text.

The most famous pattern word embeddings reveal is vector arithmetic:

$$\vec{\text{king}} - \vec{\text{man}} + \vec{\text{woman}} \approx \vec{\text{queen}}$$

This isn't a coincidence — it means the relationship "male → female" corresponds to a consistent direction in the space. Analogy becomes subtraction and addition.

To measure how similar two words are, we use cosine similarity: if $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are two word vectors, their similarity is:

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{a}\|\|\mathbf{b}\|}$$

A result near $1$ means very similar; near $0$ means unrelated.

Practice problem: Suppose embeddings give you:

$$\vec{\text{Paris}} - \vec{\text{France}} + \vec{\text{Japan}} \approx \, ?$$

What word would you expect the result to be closest to, and why? (Hint: think about what relationship the first two words share.)
