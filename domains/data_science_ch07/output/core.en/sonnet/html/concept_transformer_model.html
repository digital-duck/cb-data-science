Transformer Model

You may have heard that modern AI like ChatGPT "reads" your whole sentence before responding, rather than word by word. That intuition is right — and the mechanism behind it is called the Transformer.

The Core Idea: Attention

When you read "The bank near the river flooded," you instantly know "bank" means a riverbank, not a financial institution, because you weigh the word "river" heavily. Transformers do exactly this through a process called self-attention.

For each word (called a token), the model computes three vectors:

  • $\mathbf{q}$ — a query: "what am I looking for?"
  • $\mathbf{k}$ — a key: "what do I contain?"
  • $\mathbf{v}$ — a value: "what information do I carry?"

    • The attention score between two tokens $i$ and $j$ is:

    $$\text{score}(i, j) = \frac{\mathbf{q}_i \cdot \mathbf{k}_j}{\sqrt{d}}$$

    where $d$ is the dimension of the vectors (a scaling factor to keep numbers stable). Higher scores mean token $i$ pays more attention to token $j$. These scores are passed through a softmax so they sum to 1, then used to blend together the value vectors.

    The Pattern

    Every layer of a Transformer refines each token's representation by asking: which other tokens are most relevant to me right now? Stacking many such layers lets the model capture subtle, long-range relationships in text, code, or even images.

    Practice Problem

    A sentence has four tokens: [The, cat, sat, quietly]. Token "sat" produces query $\mathbf{q} = [1, 0]$. The keys for the four tokens are $[0,1],\,[1,1],\,[1,0],\,[0,0]$.

    Compute the raw attention score from "sat" to each token using the dot product $\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{k}_j$. Which token does "sat" attend to most?
