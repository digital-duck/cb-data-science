Natural Language Processing

You already know that computers speak in 0s and 1s, while humans speak in words, sentences, and sarcasm. Natural Language Processing — NLP for short — is the field that bridges that gap: it gives machines the tools to read, interpret, and even generate human language.

From words to numbers. Computers can't work with the word "cat" directly — they need numbers. One foundational trick is called a bag of words: represent a sentence as a vector counting how many times each word appears. If your vocabulary has $V$ words, a sentence becomes a vector in $\mathbb{R}^V$. For example, with vocabulary {cat, sat, mat, dog}, the sentence "the cat sat on the mat" maps roughly to:

$$\mathbf{x} = [1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 0]$$

This loses word order but captures what words are present — surprisingly useful for tasks like spam detection.

The pattern to notice. Once language is a vector, all the algebra you're learning applies. Two documents with similar word-count vectors are likely about similar topics. You can measure this with the cosine similarity:

$$\text{similarity} = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\|\mathbf{a}\|\,\|\mathbf{b}\|}$$

A value near $1$ means the documents are closely related; near $0$ means they share little vocabulary.

Modern NLP goes much further — transformers learn rich meaning from context — but every advanced system still builds on this core idea: represent language as math, then do math on it.
