Concept Book: Speech Recognition Tts

Data Science Ch07 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

You have spent this journey learning how machines listen and speak — how raw audio becomes tokens, how a language model assigns probabilities to word sequences, and how text gets converted back into sound through neural vocoders. Speech recognition and text-to-speech are not two separate tricks. Together they form a closed loop: sound in, meaning processed, sound out. That loop is the natural endpoint because it mirrors the way humans actually communicate — not through typed symbols, but through voice.

The loop unlocks a surprising range of real applications. A voice assistant like Siri or Alexa runs this exact cycle hundreds of millions of times a day: your speech is transcribed, a model reasons over the text, and a synthesized voice replies. Accessibility tools use the same pipeline in reverse — a screen reader converts on-screen text into speech so someone with a visual impairment can "read" a webpage. Language-learning apps close the loop tightly: they listen to your pronunciation, score it against a reference, and speak back a corrected model. Real-time translation systems chain the two steps with a translation model in the middle, so a spoken sentence in English emerges as synthesized Mandarin in under a second.

The unifying pattern is simple:

$$\text{Audio} \xrightarrow{\text{ASR}} \text{Text} \xrightarrow{\text{LM}} \text{Text} \xrightarrow{\text{TTS}} \text{Audio}$$

Any application in this family is just a different function in the middle box.
