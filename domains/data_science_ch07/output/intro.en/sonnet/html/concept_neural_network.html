Neural Network

The brain that learns by doing

Imagine you are learning to catch a ball. The first time, you miss completely. But your brain notices — too far left — and adjusts. You try again. A little better. After a hundred throws, you catch it without even thinking.

A neural network works the same way. It is a computer program built to learn from mistakes, just like you.

Inside, it has layers of tiny switches called neurons (named after brain cells). Information flows in through the first layer, passes through middle layers that transform it, and comes out the other side as an answer — like "that's a cat" or "that's a dog."

At first, the network guesses randomly. It checks if it was right, then quietly adjusts its switches to do better next time. Do this millions of times, and the network gets surprisingly good.

No one programs it to recognise cats. It figures it out from examples — the same way you learned to recognise your own face in a mirror.

Now you try: Show a friend ten photos of apples and ten of oranges. Can they guess the next one? That's neural-network learning — in human form!
