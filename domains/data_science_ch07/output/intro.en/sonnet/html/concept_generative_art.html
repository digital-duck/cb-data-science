Generative Art

The robot that draws

Imagine you gave a robot a box of colored pencils and one simple rule: "Draw a spiral, but every time you finish a loop, pick a random color." You press go — and the robot draws something you've never seen before, something you couldn't have planned yourself. That's generative art!

Why does it feel like magic?

Because you wrote the rules, but not the picture. The rules are like a recipe, and the computer follows them thousands of times, making tiny choices along the way. The result surprises even the person who wrote the recipe.

Artists have always used tools — brushes, chisels, cameras. A computer is just a new kind of tool. The artist's job is to design the rules; the computer's job is to run them and show you what they make.

The big idea

Rules + Randomness = Endless surprises.

You control the how; the computer discovers the what.

Now you try

Grab a pencil and paper. Write three rules for a drawing — like "always turn left," "make a new dot every centimeter," "flip a coin to go up or down." Follow your own rules and see what shape appears!
