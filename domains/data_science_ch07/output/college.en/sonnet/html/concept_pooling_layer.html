Pooling Layer

Definition. A pooling layer is a spatial subsampling operation applied to a feature map $F \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, where $H$, $W$, and $C$ denote height, width, and channel count respectively. Given a pooling window of size $k \times k$ and stride $s$, the layer partitions the spatial dimensions into (possibly overlapping) receptive fields and replaces each field with a single scalar summary. For max pooling, the output at position $(i, j)$ in channel $c$ is

$$P^{(c)}_{i,j} = \max_{0 \le \delta_r < k,\; 0 \le \delta_c < k} F^{(c)}_{si+\delta_r,\; sj+\delta_c}.$$

For average pooling, the max is replaced by the arithmetic mean over the same window. The output dimensions are $H' = \lfloor (H - k)/s \rfloor + 1$ and $W' = \lfloor (W - k)/s \rfloor + 1$.

Worked example. Consider a single-channel feature map

$$F = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 & 2 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 1 & 2 \\ 7 & 8 & 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 2 & 6 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$

with $k = 2$ and $s = 2$. The four non-overlapping $2 \times 2$ windows yield

$$P = \begin{pmatrix} \max(1,3,5,6) & \max(2,4,1,2) \\ \max(7,8,4,2) & \max(3,0,6,5) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 4 \\ 8 & 6 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The spatial extent is halved from $4 \times 4$ to $2 \times 2$ while the dominant activations are preserved.

Key theorem (translation invariance of max pooling). Let $F'$ be a feature map obtained from $F$ by a spatial translation of magnitude $\delta$ strictly less than the stride $s$. Then for every non-overlapping pooling window, $P(F') = P(F)$. Consequently, max pooling confers local translation invariance of order $s-1$: small positional jitter in detected features does not alter the pooled representation. This property is why pooling layers reduce sensitivity to precise object localization without discarding which features are present.

Lab cell.

import sympy as sp

F = sp.Matrix([
    [1, 3, 2, 4],
    [5, 6, 1, 2],
    [7, 8, 3, 0],
    [4, 2, 6, 5],
])

k, s = 2, 2
rows = (F.shape[0] - k) // s + 1
cols = (F.shape[1] - k) // s + 1

P = sp.zeros(rows, cols)
for i in range(rows):
    for j in range(cols):
        window = [F[i*s + dr, j*s + dc]
                  for dr in range(k) for dc in range(k)]
        P[i, j] = max(window)

sp.pprint(P)

Running this cell reproduces the $2 \times 2$ max-pooled matrix above, confirming the manual calculation.
