What the Large Language Model Achieves

Every concept developed in this text — probability distributions, entropy, gradient descent, attention mechanisms, and transformer architectures — converges at a single point: the large language model (LLM). An LLM is a parametric function $f_\theta : \mathcal{V}^* \to \Delta(\mathcal{V})$ mapping a sequence of tokens drawn from vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ to a probability distribution over the next token, where $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $d$ typically ranging from $10^8$ to $10^{12}$. Training minimises the cross-entropy loss

What makes this endpoint natural rather than arbitrary is the universality thesis: natural language is a lossy but dense encoding of human reasoning, so a model that predicts text well must implicitly recover the structure of that reasoning — syntax trees, causal chains, arithmetic identities, and domain schemata — as latent features of $\theta$.

Worked Example: Probability in Action

Consider the three-token context $\langle \texttt{the}, \texttt{integral}, \texttt{of} \rangle$. A trained LLM assigns high probability to tokens such as $\texttt{sin}$, $\texttt{x}$, and $\texttt{e}$, and near-zero probability to $\texttt{elephant}$. Formally, if $\mathbf{h}_3 = \text{Transformer}_\theta(\langle \texttt{the}, \texttt{integral}, \texttt{of} \rangle)$, then the output distribution is $\text{softmax}(\mathbf{W}_U \mathbf{h}_3)$ where $\mathbf{W}_U \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}| \times d}$ is the unembedding matrix. The model has encoded, within $\mathbf{h}_3$, a vector geometry that places mathematical continuations geometrically close to the current context.

Key Theorem (Scaling Law)

Theorem (Hoffmann et al., 2022). Under fixed compute budget $C = 6ND$ (where $N$ is parameter count and $D$ is training tokens), the optimal allocation satisfies $N^ \propto C^{0.5}$ and $D^ \propto C^{0.5}$, yielding test loss $\mathcal{L}^ \sim C^{-0.5}$. This compute-optimal* result, known as the Chinchilla scaling law, implies that loss decreases as a power law in compute — emergent capabilities appear as qualitative phase transitions along this curve.

An Invitation

The scaling law guarantees that capability grows with compute, but it says nothing about which capabilities emerge or how to elicit them. That question — how a single pretrained $f_\theta$ can be steered toward mathematics tutoring, legal reasoning, or scientific hypothesis generation — opens the door to the study of prompting, fine-tuning, and retrieval-augmented generation. Choose any one of these directions and you will find that every tool in this book, from information-theoretic bounds to attention matrices, becomes indispensable.
