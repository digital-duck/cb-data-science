Concept Book: Recommendation System

Data Science Ch01 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A recommendation system is a function $R : \mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{I} \to \mathbb{R}$ mapping user–item pairs to predicted preference scores, typically learned from a sparse observation matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{U}| \times |\mathcal{I}|}$ in which entry $M_{ui}$ is defined only when user $u$ has rated item $i$. The central task — completing $M$ from its observed entries — is an instance of low-rank matrix recovery. Under the incoherence assumptions of Candès and Recht (2009), nuclear-norm minimization recovers the true rank-$r$ matrix exactly with high probability when $\Omega(r n \log n)$ entries are observed uniformly at random. In practice, this idealization breaks down: interactions are missing-not-at-random, user and item representations are entangled with side information, and the loss landscape couples algebraic structure with distributional shift.

The concept synthesizes nearly every thread developed in the preceding chapters. Latent-factor models such as matrix factorization are the direct operationalization of low-rank structure; the stochastic gradient updates that train them are the computational substrate studied in the optimization chapters; the probabilistic matrix factorization model (Salakhutdinov and Mnih, 2008) frames the problem in the Bayesian language of prior and posterior over factor matrices $U \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times |\mathcal{U}|}$ and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times |\mathcal{I}|}$; and graph-based collaborative filtering — most visibly realized through the LightGCN architecture (He et al., 2020) — recasts user–item bipartite graphs as the domain over which spectral convolution propagates preference signals.

The field remains open in directions that are both mathematically precise and practically urgent. The exposure bias problem, in which the training distribution $p_{\text{obs}}$ differs systematically from the deployment distribution $p_{\text{target}}$, admits an inverse-propensity-score correction but introduces variance that scales as $O(1/\epsilon^2)$ when propensities are small. Debiasing and variance reduction are active research frontiers (Schnabel et al., 2016; Saito et al., 2020). Fairness constraints — demanding that $R$ satisfy demographic parity or equalized exposure across user subgroups — convert the unconstrained optimization into a constrained one whose duality gap and sample complexity are not fully characterized. Sequential recommendation, where the target is next-item prediction given an interaction history, connects to language modeling and raises questions about how Transformer attention weights relate to collaborative signals.

A self-contained investigation amenable to a graduate project begins by replicating matrix factorization on the MovieLens-1M benchmark: implement alternating least squares and stochastic gradient descent, plot the Pareto front of RMSE versus $d$, and verify that the optimal rank concentrates in the range $d \in [20, 80]$ as reported in the literature. Extend the model by incorporating user and item side features through a factorization machine layer, then introduce a held-out exposure mechanism — for instance, suppress ratings below a threshold to simulate position bias — and measure the degradation in NDCG@10. Apply an IPS correction and report the variance–bias tradeoff as propensity estimates are perturbed.

A written report of this investigation should establish: (i) a clean problem statement identifying which entries of $M$ are observed and why; (ii) a derivation of the ALS update equations from first principles; (iii) a bias analysis quantifying the gap between $\mathbb{E}[\hat{R}_{ui}]$ and the true preference under the chosen missingness model; (iv) empirical curves on at least one public dataset; and (v) a discussion of one open question — fairness, robustness to distribution shift, or scalability to $|\mathcal{I}| \sim 10^7$ — that the experiment illuminates but does not resolve. The last point is not a formality: it is the invitation to treat recommendation not as a deployed product but as a research object whose geometry, dynamics, and social consequences remain incompletely understood.
