Data Collection

The Bug Jar Experiment

Imagine you and your friends want to answer a big question: "Which bugs live in our school garden?" You could just guess — but guesses aren't very reliable. Instead, you grab some jars and spend an afternoon actually looking, catching, counting, and writing down what you find. That careful looking-and-writing is data collection.

Why does this feel right? Answers based on real observations are stronger than answers based on feelings. When you write down "3 ladybugs, 7 ants, 1 butterfly," those marks become evidence — something you can show someone else and they can check.

The key idea Data collection means gathering real observations or measurements about the world in an organized way, so you can learn something true from them. Good data collection is fair (you check every corner, not just the sunny spot), consistent (everyone counts the same way), and recorded (written down before you forget!).

Now you try Pick one question — "How many cars are red on our street?" or "How many students bring lunch vs. buy lunch?" — then design a simple tally sheet and collect your own data for one day.
