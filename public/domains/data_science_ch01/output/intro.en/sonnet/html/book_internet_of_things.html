Payoff

Imagine your backpack could whisper, "You left me at school!" Imagine your plants could text, "I'm thirsty!" Imagine the whole world around you — chairs, bridges, traffic lights, even shoes — quietly talking to each other and to you.

That is exactly what the Internet of Things makes real.

All along this journey, you learned how things sense, remember, and share. A temperature sensor notices warmth. A tiny computer decides what to do. A wireless signal carries the message across the room — or across the planet. Put those ideas together and every thing becomes a little brain connected to every other little brain.

The Internet of Things is not one invention. It is the moment all those smaller ideas finally shake hands. Smart homes, hospitals that watch over patients while they sleep, farms that water crops only when the soil is dry, cities that turn streetlights on only when someone walks by — each one is just our earlier concepts wearing a new costume.

The journey does not end here; it starts here.

Now you try: pick one object in your home and dream up one question it could answer if it could talk. What would it say?
