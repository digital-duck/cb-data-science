Artificial Intelligence

You've probably heard that AI can write poems, recognize faces, or beat humans at chess. But what is it, actually, doing under the hood?

At its core, AI is about building systems that learn patterns from data and use those patterns to make decisions. Unlike a regular program — which follows explicit rules a programmer wrote — an AI program discovers its own rules by studying examples.

From Examples to Rules

The most common approach is called supervised learning. You feed the system a large set of labeled examples: thousands of images tagged "cat" or "not cat." The system adjusts internal numbers called weights (written $w_1, w_2, \ldots, w_n$) until its predictions match the labels. The goal is to minimize a loss function $L$, which measures how wrong the system is:

$$L = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2$$

Here $y_i$ is the correct answer, $\hat{y}_i$ is the system's guess, and $N$ is the number of examples. A smaller $L$ means better predictions.

The Simple Pattern

AI training follows a loop: predict → measure error → adjust weights → repeat. Each pass through the data nudges the weights in a direction that reduces $L$. After thousands of passes, the system generalizes — it can handle examples it has never seen before.

Practice Problem

A simple AI is predicting tomorrow's temperature. For three days it guesses $\hat{y} = [70, 68, 75]\ ^\circ\text{F}$, but the real temperatures were $y = [72, 65, 75]\ ^\circ\text{F}$.

Calculate the loss $L$ using the formula above. Is the AI doing well? What would a perfect prediction give for $L$?
