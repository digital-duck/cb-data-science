Speech Recognition Tts

You already know the basic idea: your phone "hears" you and turns your words into text, or reads a message out loud to you. What's actually happening under the hood is more interesting than magic — it's signal processing plus pattern matching, and the two directions are nearly mirror images of each other.

From sound to text (ASR). A microphone captures air-pressure changes as a waveform — a list of amplitude values sampled thousands of times per second. The system slices that waveform into short overlapping windows (typically 25 ms each) and extracts a compact fingerprint from each window called a mel-frequency cepstral coefficient (MFCC) vector. If a window has $D$ features, a whole utterance becomes a matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times D}$, where $T$ is the number of time steps. A neural network (often a transformer) maps this matrix to a sequence of tokens — first phonemes, then words.

From text to speech (TTS). The pipeline reverses: text is converted to phonemes using pronunciation rules, then a neural vocoder synthesizes a waveform that matches those phonemes with natural-sounding pitch and rhythm. Models like WaveNet predict each audio sample conditioned on the samples before it, producing surprisingly human output.

The key pattern. Both directions lean on the same idea: break the signal into small chunks, extract features from each chunk, and let a sequence model handle the ordering. The feature vector is the shared currency.
