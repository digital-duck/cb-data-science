Speech Recognition Tts

Have you ever talked to a smart speaker and watched it write down every word you said? Or heard a computer read a story out loud in a human-sounding voice? That feels almost like magic — but it is actually a skill computers had to learn, just like you learned to read and talk.

Here is the intuition. Your voice is really just wiggly air. When you say "hello," your mouth pushes air in a special wiggly pattern. A microphone catches those wiggles. Speech recognition is the computer's job of turning wiggles → words. It has heard millions of voices, so it recognises patterns the way you recognise a friend's face. Going the other way, text-to-speech (TTS) starts with written words and builds wiggles back — the computer has learned how lips and tongues shape every sound, so it can imitate a voice.

Think of it as two translators working in opposite directions:

$$\text{Sound waves} \xrightarrow{\text{speech recognition}} \text{Words} \xrightarrow{\text{TTS}} \text{Sound waves}$$

Now you try. Record yourself saying a colour, then play it back in slow motion. Can you see the waveform going up and down? That shape is your voice — and that is exactly what the computer is reading.
