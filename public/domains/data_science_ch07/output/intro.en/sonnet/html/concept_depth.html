Depth

Imagine you drop a coin into a swimming pool. You watch it spin and sink — down, down, down — until it rests on the bottom. How far did it fall? That distance from the surface to the bottom is called depth.

Now think about a family tree. Your mom is one step above you. Your grandma is one step above your mom. If someone asked "how deep is Grandma in this family?", you'd count: two steps down from the top. Depth is just counting how many steps you are from where things began.

This idea shows up everywhere: how many floors underground a subway goes, how many questions deep you have to ask before you reach the simplest answer, how many layers an onion has.

The key insight is this — depth tells you how far from the start you are, measured in steps, not in centimetres or miles.

We write the depth of something as a number. The very top — the surface, the root, the beginning — always has depth 0. One step below is depth 1, two steps is depth 2, and so on.
