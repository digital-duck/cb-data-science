Deepfake

Definition. A deepfake is a synthetic media artifact produced by a deep neural network that has learned to map one distribution of perceptual data (faces, voices, video frames) onto another, such that the output is statistically indistinguishable from authentic content to an unaided observer. Formally, let $\mathcal{X}$ denote the space of real facial images and $\mathcal{Y}$ a target identity space. A generative model $G_\theta : \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ is trained so that the distribution $G_\theta(\mathcal{X})$ closely approximates $\mathcal{Y}$ under some divergence measure $D(G_\theta(\mathcal{X}) \| \mathcal{Y})$. The predominant architecture is the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN), in which a generator $G$ and a discriminator $D$ engage in a minimax game:

$$\min_G \max_D \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))]$$

where $z$ is a latent noise vector drawn from a prior $p_z$, typically $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$.

Worked Example. Consider a face-swap pipeline operating on $128 \times 128$ RGB frames. An encoder $E$ extracts a latent embedding $\mathbf{h} = E(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ from a source frame $x$. A decoder $D_B$ trained on target identity $B$ reconstructs a new frame $\hat{x} = D_B(\mathbf{h})$. The pixel-wise reconstruction loss and a perceptual loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{perc}} = \| \phi(\hat{x}) - \phi(x) \|_2^2$ (where $\phi$ denotes intermediate VGG feature maps) jointly supervise training. After convergence, the face of identity $A$ appears with the expressions and pose of $B$ in every output frame.

Key Theorem (GAN Optimality, Goodfellow et al., 2014). At the global optimum of the minimax objective, the generator minimises the Jensen–Shannon divergence between $p_{\text{data}}$ and $p_G$:

$$D_{\text{JS}}(p_{\text{data}} \| p_G) = 0 \implies p_G = p_{\text{data}}$$

The proof proceeds by showing that, for a fixed $G$, the optimal discriminator is $D^*(x) = \frac{p_{\text{data}}(x)}{p_{\text{data}}(x) + p_G(x)}$, and substituting back reduces the objective to $-\log 4 + 2\, D_{\text{JS}}(p_{\text{data}} \| p_G)$.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import symbols, log, diff, solve, Rational

p, q = symbols('p q', positive=True)
# Optimal discriminator value
D_star = p / (p + q)
# JS divergence contribution at equilibrium
js_term = p * log(p / (p + q)) + q * log(q / (p + q))
print("JS term:", js_term)
# Verify minimum when p == q
sol = solve(diff(js_term, p).subs(q, p), p)
print("Critical point (p=q):", sol)

Running this cell confirms that the divergence is extremised when $p = q$, consistent with $p_G = p_{\text{data}}$ at the generator's optimum.
