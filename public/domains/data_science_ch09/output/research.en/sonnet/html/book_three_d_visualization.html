Payoff

Three-dimensional visualization is not merely a presentational convenience—it is the epistemological terminus of a computational pipeline that begins with symbolic representation, passes through algebraic manipulation and numerical approximation, and ends only when abstract structure has been rendered perceptible to human intuition. The central claim of this capstone is that the map $\Phi: \mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{R}^3$, which embeds a symbolic object $s \in \mathcal{S}$ into Euclidean three-space, is itself a mathematical object worthy of rigorous study: its smoothness, injectivity, and stability under perturbation of $s$ determine whether the visualization is faithful in a precisely quantifiable sense.

Theorem (Faithfulness Criterion). Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a family of symbolic expressions parameterized by $\theta \in \Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^k$, and let $\Phi_\theta: D \subset \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$ be the scalar field induced by evaluating the expression at $\theta$. The isosurface $\Sigma_\theta = \Phi_\theta^{-1}(c)$ changes topology at parameter values where $\nabla \Phi_\theta = 0$ on $\Sigma_\theta$; all other parameter values constitute a smooth stratum in which deformation is isotopic. This is a direct consequence of the Implicit Function Theorem applied fiberwise, and the bifurcation locus is precisely the discriminant variety of $\Phi$.

Proof sketch. Fix $c$ a regular value of $\Phi_{\theta_0}$, so $\nabla \Phi_{\theta_0} \neq 0$ on $\Sigma_{\theta_0}$. By the IFT, there exists a neighborhood $U \ni \theta_0$ in $\Theta$ and a smooth family of embeddings $\iota_\theta: \Sigma_{\theta_0} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$ such that $\iota_\theta(\Sigma_{\theta_0}) = \Sigma_\theta$ for $\theta \in U$. Topology changes only when $c$ ceases to be regular, i.e., when $(\theta, x) \in \Theta \times D$ satisfies $\Phi_\theta(x) = c$ and $\nabla_x \Phi_\theta(x) = 0$ simultaneously. $\square$

This result, classical in singularity theory (see Arnol'd, Catastrophe Theory, 1984; Milnor, Morse Theory, 1963), acquires fresh urgency in the symbolic-computation context because $\Phi_\theta$ is not given by a fixed analytic formula but is generated algorithmically, making the discriminant variety computable only approximately. The open problem—active in the intersection of certified numerics and real algebraic geometry—is to provide rigorous enclosures for the discriminant locus from an interval-arithmetic evaluation of a computer-algebra expression.

Literature context. Marching-cubes algorithms (Lorensen & Cline, 1987) implicitly assume regularity of $c$; their notorious topological ambiguities occur precisely at discriminant-variety crossings. Certified isosurface extraction (Plantinga & Vegter, 2004) addresses this via subdivision, but the connection to the discriminant variety is seldom made explicit in the visualization literature. On the symbolic side, Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition (Collins, 1975) can, in principle, stratify $\Theta$ by topology type, but the doubly-exponential complexity makes it impractical for real-time visualization of high-degree systems—here lies a genuine open gap.

Suggested investigation. Choose a one-parameter family $\Phi_t(x,y,z) = f(x,y,z) + t \cdot g(x,y,z)$ where $f$ and $g$ are low-degree polynomials with a known critical manifold. Animate the isosurface over $t \in [0,1]$ and identify, both visually and by solving $\Phi_t = c$, $\nabla \Phi_t = 0$ simultaneously (e.g., via a Gröbner basis computation in SageMath), each topological transition. Compare the certified transition values against the frame where the visual artifact first appears in a marching-cubes render. Quantify the discrepancy as a function of grid resolution.

Report-writing note. A rigorous write-up of this investigation must establish: (i) the algebraic certificate for each bifurcation value $t^$, with residual bounds; (ii) the topological invariant (Euler characteristic, Betti numbers via persistent homology) before and after each transition; (iii) the grid-resolution scaling law for the visual artifact onset; and (iv) a statement of whether the marching-cubes ambiguity at $t^$ is resolvable by asymptotic cone analysis or requires subdivision. This structure transforms a rendering exercise into a contribution at the boundary of certified geometric computation and visual communication—precisely the intellectual territory three-dimensional visualization, understood rigorously, is designed to open.
