Noise

Definition. In the context of signal processing and probability theory, noise refers to any random or deterministic disturbance that corrupts an observed signal. Formally, let $s(t)$ denote a deterministic signal and $\eta(t)$ a random process. The observed signal is modeled as

$$x(t) = s(t) + \eta(t),$$

where $\eta(t)$ is characterized by its probability distribution, mean, and power spectral density. The most common assumption is additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN), in which $\eta(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ independently at each time step, meaning the noise has zero mean, variance $\sigma^2$, and a flat power spectrum across all frequencies.

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) quantifies the relative strength of the signal versus the noise:

$$\mathrm{SNR} = \frac{P_s}{P_n} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[s(t)^2]}{\mathbb{E}[\eta(t)^2]},$$

often expressed in decibels as $\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}(\mathrm{SNR})$.

Worked Example. Suppose $s(t) = \sin(2\pi t)$ is sampled at $N = 100$ equally spaced points on $[0, 1]$, and corrupted by AWGN with $\sigma = 0.5$. The signal power is $P_s = \tfrac{1}{2}$ (the mean square of a sine wave over a full period), and the noise power is $P_n = \sigma^2 = 0.25$. Thus

$$\mathrm{SNR} = \frac{0.5}{0.25} = 2, \qquad \mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{dB}} = 10\log_{10}(2) \approx 3.01 \text{ dB}.$$

Key Theorem (Central Limit Theorem for Noise). If $\eta(t)$ is formed as the sum of $n$ independent, identically distributed random variables each with mean $0$ and variance $\sigma_0^2$, then as $n \to \infty$,

$$\eta(t) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\, n\sigma_0^2).$$

This theorem justifies the Gaussian noise model in practice: physical noise arising from many independent microscopic sources (thermal fluctuations, shot noise) is well approximated by a Gaussian distribution.

Lab Cell.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from sympy import *

t_vals = np.linspace(0, 1, 100)
s = np.sin(2 * np.pi * t_vals)
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
eta = rng.normal(0, 0.5, size=100)
x = s + eta

signal_power = np.mean(s**2)
noise_power  = np.mean(eta**2)
snr_db = 10 * np.log10(signal_power / noise_power)
print(f"SNR = {snr_db:.2f} dB")

plt.plot(t_vals, s, label="Clean signal")
plt.plot(t_vals, x, alpha=0.6, label="Noisy observation")
plt.legend(); plt.xlabel("t"); plt.title("Signal vs. Noisy Observation")
plt.show()
