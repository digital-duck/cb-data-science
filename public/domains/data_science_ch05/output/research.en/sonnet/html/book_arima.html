What ARIMA Achieves

The ARIMA$(p,d,q)$ model unifies three distinct mechanisms of temporal dependence into a single, estimable class. Let $\{Y_t\}$ be an observed time series. Define the difference operator $\nabla^d Y_t = (1-B)^d Y_t$, where $B$ denotes the backshift operator. ARIMA posits that $\nabla^d Y_t = X_t$ satisfies the ARMA equation

$$ \Phi(B)\,X_t = \Theta(B)\,\varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{WN}(0,\sigma^2), $$

where $\Phi(B) = 1 - \phi_1 B - \cdots - \phi_p B^p$ and $\Theta(B) = 1 + \theta_1 B + \cdots + \theta_q B^q$ have roots strictly outside the unit disk. The integer $d$ is the order of integration: $Y_t \sim \mathrm{I}(d)$ if and only if $\nabla^d Y_t$ is $\mathrm{I}(0)$, i.e., stationary and invertible. This formulation is the natural endpoint of a ladder that passes through stationarity, autocorrelation structure, and spectral representation: ARIMA is the minimal parametric model that is closed under differencing, admits Wold's decomposition after reduction, and yields finite-variance forecasts with computable prediction intervals.

The landmark result formalizing this endpoint is the Wold Decomposition Theorem combined with the Box–Jenkins identification and estimation framework (Box, Jenkins, Reinsel, and Ljung, Time Series Analysis, 5th ed., 2015). Invertibility of $\Theta(B)$ guarantees the infinite AR representation $\Pi(B)X_t = \varepsilon_t$, enabling consistent estimation of $(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_p,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_q)$ by conditional or exact maximum likelihood. The information criteria of Akaike (AIC) and Schwarz (BIC) furnish model-order selection with known asymptotic properties; consistency of BIC for ARMA order selection is proved under mild moment conditions in Hannan and Quinn (1979) and refined by Tsay and Tiao (1984).

Connection to Application Domains

Because the ARIMA family spans non-stationary processes through differencing and captures both short-memory (AR) and shock-propagation (MA) dynamics, it serves as the backbone of quantitative analysis wherever observations arrive sequentially. Four domains are worth singling out.

Macroeconomics and finance. Nelson and Plosser (1982) showed that most U.S. macroeconomic aggregates — real GNP, the price level, nominal interest rates — are better modelled as $\mathrm{I}(1)$ processes than as trend-stationary ones, making ARIMA$(p,1,q)$ the default benchmark. Asset-return modelling uses ARIMA for the conditional mean, feeding residuals into GARCH for the conditional variance: the two-stage Box–Jenkins/Engle pipeline remains the practitioner standard. Open research questions include order-selection stability under structural breaks (Perron, 1989) and whether persistent volatility in cryptocurrency markets violates the finite-variance assumption embedded in ARMA theory.

Signal processing and control. In discrete-time control, the ARIMA model corresponds to the pulse-transfer function of a plant with integrating action; the $d$ integrations appear as poles at $z=1$. Kalman filter recursions for the ARIMA state-space form (Harvey, Forecasting, Structural Time Series Models and the Kalman Filter, 1990) provide the minimum-mean-square-error one-step predictor $\hat{Y}_{t+1|t}$ and its error variance $P_{t+1|t}$ in $O(n)$ time, a fact that underpins real-time forecasting in embedded systems. The connection between ARIMA and the Holt–Winters exponential smoothing family (ARIMA$(0,2,2)$ corresponds exactly to Holt's linear method) shows that much of classical smoothing theory is subsumed by the ARIMA framework.

Environmental and geophysical sciences. Temperature anomaly records, river discharge, and atmospheric $\mathrm{CO}_2$ concentrations exhibit trends and seasonal cycles that seasonal ARIMA (SARIMA$(p,d,q)(P,D,Q)_s$) handles through both non-seasonal and seasonal differencing. The IPCC assessment reports routinely cite ARIMA-family baseline forecasts against which machine-learning surrogates are evaluated; understanding why neural-network methods sometimes fail to outperform ARIMA on short, noisy geophysical records (Makridakis et al., M4 Competition, 2020) is an active methodological question.

Public health and epidemiology. Surveillance systems — influenza-like illness, hospital admissions, wastewater pathogen concentration — use ARIMA as the null model against which epidemic signals are detected. The CDC's Farrington algorithm and its successors are generalized ARIMA thresholding procedures. Parameter non-stationarity during outbreaks (the process changes mid-sample) stresses the standard theory and motivates adaptive or piecewise-ARIMA approaches that remain an area of active development.

Invitation to Investigate

Each domain above contains a concrete open question accessible to a graduate student with one to two semesters of time series coursework. The most self-contained entry point is the M4 Forecasting Competition dataset (Makridakis et al., 2020, International Journal of Forecasting): 100,000 series across six frequencies, with public train/test splits and reported errors for 61 submitted methods. An investigation would proceed as follows. First, fit ARIMA$(p,d,q)$ to a stratified sample of the M4 series using AIC-selected orders, record the symmetric mean absolute percentage error (sMAPE) and mean absolute scaled error (MASE) on the holdout. Second, compare against a simple seasonal naïve baseline and against one neural benchmark (e.g., N-BEATS or DeepAR). Third, disaggregate by series length, frequency, and coefficient of variation to determine when ARIMA loses and why — whether the failure traces to order-selection bias, structural breaks, or non-Gaussian innovations. A write-up of this investigation should establish: the empirical distribution of selected $(p,d,q)$ orders across domains; a regression of forecast error on series length and estimated integration order; and a statement of at least one setting in which ARIMA achieves competitive or superior accuracy to neural methods, with a statistical test (e.g., Diebold–Mariano) supporting the claim. This is not a confirmatory exercise — the result may well favor neural approaches in aggregate — but the discipline of grounding a machine-learning comparison in a rigorously estimated ARIMA benchmark is the professional norm, and learning to do it carefully is the intellectual payoff of everything that precedes this chapter.
