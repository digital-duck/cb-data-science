Moving Average Model

Definition. A moving average model of order $q$, denoted $\mathrm{MA}(q)$, is the process

$$X_t = \mu + \varepsilon_t + \theta_1 \varepsilon_{t-1} + \cdots + \theta_q \varepsilon_{t-q}, \qquad \varepsilon_t \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2),$$

where $\{\theta_j\}_{j=1}^q \subset \mathbb{R}$ are the MA coefficients. In backshift notation, $X_t - \mu = \Theta(B)\,\varepsilon_t$ with $\Theta(z) = 1 + \theta_1 z + \cdots + \theta_q z^q$.

Theorem (Stationarity). Every $\mathrm{MA}(q)$ process is weakly stationary regardless of the values of $\{\theta_j\}$. The autocovariance function satisfies

$$\gamma(h) = \sigma^2 \sum_{j=0}^{q-|h|} \theta_j \theta_{j+|h|}, \quad |h| \leq q; \qquad \gamma(h) = 0, \quad |h| > q,$$

with $\theta_0 \equiv 1$. Proof sketch. Since $X_t - \mu$ is a finite linear combination of i.i.d. zero-mean variables, $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mu$ and $\gamma(h)$ depends only on $|h|$; direct substitution yields the formula. $\square$

A structural caveat: the coefficients $\theta$ and $\theta^{-1}$ produce identical $\{\gamma(h)\}$, so the MA polynomial $\Theta(z)$ is not identifiable from second-order structure alone. Restricting all roots of $\Theta(z)$ to lie outside the unit disk selects the canonical invertible representative; non-invertibility degrades forecasting even when autocovariances match.

Literature context. The MA model originates in Yule (1927) and was systematized by Box and Jenkins (1970). The classical identification strategy examines the ACF for a hard cutoff at lag $q$, which is theoretically exact but brittle in finite samples. Open problems include: MLE for MA parameters yields a non-convex likelihood surface with local optima near the invertibility boundary; model selection criteria (AIC, BIC) degrade under heavy-tailed noise.

Suggested investigation. Simulate $\mathrm{MA}(1)$ processes at $\theta = 0.8$ and its dual $\theta^{-1} = 1.25$ for $n \in \{50, 200, 1000\}$. Fit both candidates by conditional and exact MLE using statsmodels or R's arima(). Measure parameter recovery bias and forecasting RMSE, then repeat with Student-$t_3$ noise to test Gaussian MLE robustness near the unit-root boundary.

Report-writing note. A write-up should establish: the theoretical ACF cutoff, empirical ACF plots confirming or contradicting it, a comparison table of estimates and forecast errors across $\theta/\theta^{-1}$ pairs and sample sizes, and a discussion of whether enforcing invertibility during optimization consistently selects the correct branch. The investigation is complete when it can articulate why invertibility matters for forecasting even when $\{\gamma(h)\}$ is identical between dual solutions.
