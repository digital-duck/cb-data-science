Forecasting

Definition. Forecasting is the process of estimating the value of a quantity at a future time $t + h$ given observations up to and including time $t$. Formally, let $\{y_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$ be a discrete time series. An $h$-step-ahead point forecast is a function $\hat{y}_{T+h|T} = f(y_T, y_{T-1}, \ldots)$ that minimises a chosen loss, most commonly the mean squared error $\mathbb{E}[(y_{T+h} - \hat{y}_{T+h|T})^2]$. A prediction interval accompanies the point forecast to express uncertainty: $[\hat{y}_{T+h|T} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\,\hat{\sigma}_h]$, where $\hat{\sigma}_h$ is the estimated $h$-step forecast standard deviation and $z_{\alpha/2}$ is the standard normal critical value for coverage $1-\alpha$.

Worked Example. Consider monthly average temperatures (°C) recorded over 24 months. Suppose the series exhibits a linear trend $\mu_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t$ with additive white noise $\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, so $y_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t + \varepsilon_t$. Ordinary least squares yields $\hat{\beta}_0 = 5.2$ and $\hat{\beta}_1 = 0.3$ from the 24 observations. The 3-step-ahead forecast is:

$$\hat{y}_{27|24} = 5.2 + 0.3 \times 27 = 13.3 \text{ °C.}$$

With residual standard deviation $\hat{\sigma} = 1.1$ °C and $z_{0.025} = 1.96$, the 95 % prediction interval is $[13.3 \pm 2.16] = [11.14,\ 15.46]$ °C.

Key Theorem (Unbiasedness of the Linear Trend Forecast). Let $y_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t + \varepsilon_t$ with $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t] = 0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_t) = \sigma^2 < \infty$. The OLS forecast $\hat{y}_{T+h|T} = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1(T+h)$ is unbiased: $\mathbb{E}[\hat{y}_{T+h|T}] = \beta_0 + \beta_1(T+h)$. Moreover, among all linear unbiased forecasts formed from $\{y_1, \ldots, y_T\}$, it achieves the smallest mean squared forecast error. The proof follows directly from the Gauss–Markov theorem applied to the design matrix evaluated at the future time point $T + h$.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

import sympy as sp

t, beta0, beta1 = sp.symbols('t beta0 beta1')
trend = beta0 + beta1 * t
forecast = trend.subs([(beta0, sp.Rational(26, 5)),
                       (beta1, sp.Rational(3, 10)),
                       (t, 27)])
print("Point forecast:", forecast)          # 13.3
sigma_h = sp.Rational(11, 10)
z = sp.Rational(196, 100)
lower = forecast - z * sigma_h
upper = forecast + z * sigma_h
print(f"95% PI: [{lower}, {upper}]")        # [11.144, 15.456]
