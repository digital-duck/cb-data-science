Regulatory Compliance

Definition. Let $\mathcal{S} = (Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_0, F)$ be a finite automaton representing the state-transition semantics of a computational system, and let $\mathcal{R} = \{\varphi_1, \ldots, \varphi_k\}$ be a finite set of regulatory constraints expressed in linear temporal logic (LTL). The system $\mathcal{S}$ is compliant with $\mathcal{R}$ if and only if every execution trace $\pi \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S})$ satisfies $\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k} \varphi_i$. In practice, $\mathcal{R}$ encodes requirements from instruments such as GDPR Article 22, the EU AI Act Annex III, or HIPAA §164.312, each translatable to safety or liveness properties over the system's observable behavior.

Theorem (Compliance Verification Complexity). Model-checking $\mathcal{S} \models \varphi$ for $\varphi \in$ LTL is PSPACE-complete in the size of $\varphi$ and $\mathcal{S}$ (Clarke et al., 1999). Consequently, exhaustive compliance verification is intractable for large state spaces, motivating abstraction-refinement and runtime monitoring as practical alternatives.

Literature context. The tension between expressiveness and decidability is central to regulatory engineering. Basin, Harvan & Klaedtke (2012) propose monitorable trace-based compliance. More recently, Governatori et al. (2023) examine normative systems over deontic logic, while Wachter, Mittelstadt & Russell (2017) raise the open problem of counterfactual compliance—whether a system that violates $\varphi$ can be repaired minimally. For LLM pipelines, compliance over stochastic traces remains largely unresolved.

Suggested investigation. Encode a representative regulatory clause (e.g., GDPR right-to-explanation) as an LTL formula $\varphi$, implement a runtime monitor over an LLM workflow execution log, and measure the false-positive rate under model non-determinism. Vary $|\mathcal{R}|$ and record verification time empirically against the PSPACE lower bound.

Report-writing note. A write-up should formally state the monitored property, justify the LTL encoding against the natural-language clause, report precision/recall of the monitor, and discuss whether observed violations are attributable to specification ambiguity or genuine system non-compliance.
