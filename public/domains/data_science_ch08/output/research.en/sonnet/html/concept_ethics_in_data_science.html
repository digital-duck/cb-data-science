Ethics In Data Science

Definition. Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ be a dataset drawn from joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ over feature space $\mathcal{X}$ and label space $\mathcal{Y}$, and let $h: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ be a learned predictor. Ethical data science demands that $h$ satisfy normative constraints — fairness, privacy, transparency — alongside predictive accuracy $\mathbb{E}[\ell(h(X), Y)]$, where $\ell$ is a task loss.

Theorem (Impossibility; Chouldechova 2017). For a binary classifier with base rates $p_A \neq p_B$ across groups $A, B$, it is impossible to simultaneously satisfy calibration within groups, equal false positive rates, and equal false negative rates. That is, no $h$ can achieve all three constraints unless $p_A = p_B$.

Proof sketch. Express each constraint as a linear equation in the confusion-matrix entries conditional on group membership. Under unequal base rates the system is overdetermined; a standard counting argument yields a contradiction. $\square$

This result formalizes a tension that practitioners encounter empirically: optimizing for one fairness criterion degrades another. The literature distinguishes individual fairness (Dwork et al. 2012), group fairness (demographic parity, equalized odds), and causal fairness via do-calculus (Kusner et al. 2017). Privacy adds a further constraint: $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-differential privacy bounds information leakage but provably degrades accuracy in proportion to dataset sensitivity (Dwork & Roth 2014). Transparency requirements (explainability) remain theoretically under-specified relative to fairness and privacy.

Investigation. Replicate the impossibility proof on the COMPAS recidivism dataset: compute calibration curves and error-rate parity simultaneously; quantify the trade-off surface as base-rate disparity $|p_A - p_B|$ varies via subsampling.

Write-up. A complete report should state which fairness criteria were targeted and why, derive the constraint equations for your classifier family, report the empirical trade-off curve with confidence intervals, and discuss which criterion aligns with the decision-maker's normative commitments and relevant regulatory guidance (e.g., EU AI Act Article 10).
