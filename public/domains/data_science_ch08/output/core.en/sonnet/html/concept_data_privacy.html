Data Privacy

Definition. Data privacy refers to the set of mathematical and computational guarantees that control what information an adversary can infer about an individual from a published dataset or query result. The most rigorous modern formulation is differential privacy (Dwork, 2006). A randomized mechanism $\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{R}$ is said to be $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-differentially private if, for every pair of neighboring datasets $D, D' \in \mathcal{D}$ differing in exactly one record, and for every measurable output set $S \subseteq \mathcal{R}$,

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in S] \;\leq\; e^{\varepsilon}\,\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in S] + \delta.$$

When $\delta = 0$ the guarantee is called pure differential privacy. The parameter $\varepsilon > 0$ is the privacy budget: smaller values impose stricter privacy at the cost of greater output noise.

Worked Example. Suppose a hospital wishes to publish the mean age $\mu$ of its $n = 1000$ patients, where each age lies in $[0, 120]$. The sensitivity of the mean query is

$$\Delta f = \frac{120 - 0}{n} = \frac{120}{1000} = 0.12,$$

because replacing one record can shift the mean by at most $0.12$ years. The Laplace mechanism releases $\tilde{\mu} = \mu + \mathrm{Lap}(0,\, \Delta f / \varepsilon)$. Setting $\varepsilon = 0.5$ yields noise drawn from $\mathrm{Lap}(0, 0.24)$, whose standard deviation is $0.24\sqrt{2} \approx 0.34$ years — a clinically negligible perturbation that nonetheless satisfies $(0.5, 0)$-differential privacy.

Key Theorem (Composition). If $\mathcal{M}_1$ is $(\varepsilon_1, \delta_1)$-DP and $\mathcal{M}_2$ is $(\varepsilon_2, \delta_2)$-DP, then their sequential composition $(\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2)$ is $(\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2,\, \delta_1 + \delta_2)$-DP. This sequential composition theorem implies that privacy budgets accumulate across queries, so practitioners must track total expenditure $\sum_i \varepsilon_i$ across the lifetime of a dataset.

# Simulate the hospital example
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
true_mean = 45.3                      # years
sensitivity, epsilon = 0.12, 0.5
noise = rng.laplace(scale=sensitivity / epsilon, size=10_000)
print(f"Mean released value: {true_mean + noise.mean():.4f}")
