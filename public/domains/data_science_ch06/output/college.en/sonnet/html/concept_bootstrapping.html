Bootstrapping

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be an independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sample drawn from an unknown population distribution $F$. The bootstrap is a resampling method that approximates the sampling distribution of a statistic $\hat{\theta} = g(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ by treating the empirical distribution $\hat{F}_n$ as a surrogate for $F$. A bootstrap sample $X_1^, X_2^, \ldots, X_n^$ is drawn with replacement from $\{X_1, \ldots, X_n\}$, and the bootstrap replicate $\hat{\theta}^ = g(X_1^, \ldots, X_n^)$ is computed. Repeating this procedure $B$ times produces replicates $\hat{\theta}^{(1)}, \ldots, \hat{\theta}^{(B)}$, whose empirical distribution estimates the true sampling distribution of $\hat{\theta}$.

Worked Example. Suppose $n = 6$ observations of exam scores are $\{72, 85, 90, 68, 77, 83\}$, and we wish to estimate the standard error of the sample median $\tilde{X}$. No closed-form formula exists for $\text{SE}(\tilde{X})$ under a general $F$. We draw $B = 5000$ bootstrap samples with replacement. For each sample, we record the median. The bootstrap standard error is then

$$\widehat{\text{SE}}_B = \sqrt{\frac{1}{B-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\left(\hat{\theta}^{*(b)} - \bar{\hat{\theta}}^*\right)^2},$$

where $\bar{\hat{\theta}}^ = B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\hat{\theta}^{(b)}$.

Key Theorem (Bootstrap Consistency). Under mild regularity conditions on $g$ and $F$, the bootstrap distribution of $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}^* - \hat{\theta})$ converges weakly to the same limiting distribution as $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta} - \theta)$ as $n \to \infty$. Formally,

$$\sup_{t \in \mathbb{R}}\left|P^*\!\left(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}^* - \hat{\theta}) \le t\right) - P\!\left(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta} - \theta) \le t\right)\right| \xrightarrow{p} 0,$$

where $P^$ denotes probability under the bootstrap resampling mechanism. This result, due to Efron (1979) and refined by Bickel and Freedman (1981), justifies constructing confidence intervals as $\left[\hat{\theta} - z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\widehat{\text{SE}}_B,\; \hat{\theta} + z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\widehat{\text{SE}}_B\right]$ or, more accurately, via the percentile method using empirical quantiles of $\{\hat{\theta}^{(b)}\}$.

Lab Cell (SymPy / NumPy).

import numpy as np

rng   = np.random.default_rng(42)
data  = np.array([72, 85, 90, 68, 77, 83])
B     = 5_000

replicates = [
    np.median(rng.choice(data, size=len(data), replace=True))
    for _ in range(B)
]
se_boot = np.std(replicates, ddof=1)
ci_lo, ci_hi = np.percentile(replicates, [2.5, 97.5])

print(f"Bootstrap SE of median : {se_boot:.3f}")
print(f"95% percentile CI      : ({ci_lo:.1f}, {ci_hi:.1f})")
