Concept Book: Data Mining

Payoff

Data mining is the computational discipline of extracting previously unknown, valid, and actionable patterns from large datasets by combining methods from statistics, linear algebra, and algorithmic complexity theory. Formally, given a dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ where $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $y_i \in \mathcal{Y}$, data mining seeks a function $f : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathcal{Y}$ that minimizes a loss $\mathcal{L}(f, \mathcal{D})$ while simultaneously satisfying a generalization constraint, typically expressed through a regularization term $\lambda \Omega(f)$. The joint minimization

$$\hat{f} = \arg\min_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \left[ \mathcal{L}(f, \mathcal{D}) + \lambda \Omega(f) \right]$$

unifies supervised learning, clustering, and association-rule discovery under a single variational framework.

Worked example. Consider $n = 100$ student records, each carrying two features: weekly study hours $x_1$ and prior GPA $x_2$. Setting $\mathcal{L}$ to mean-squared error and $\Omega(f) = \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$ (ridge penalty) yields the closed-form solution $\hat{\mathbf{w}} = (X^\top X + \lambda I)^{-1} X^\top \mathbf{y}$. With $\lambda = 0.1$ one finds that $\hat{w}_1 \approx 0.42$ and $\hat{w}_2 \approx 1.17$, quantifying each feature's predictive contribution and revealing that prior GPA carries nearly three times the weight of study time in this cohort.

Theorem (Bias–Variance Decomposition). For any estimator $\hat{f}$ and unseen point $\mathbf{x}^*$, the expected prediction error decomposes as

$$\mathbb{E}\left[(y^* - \hat{f}(\mathbf{x}^*))^2\right] = \underbrace{\mathrm{Bias}^2[\hat{f}]}_{\text{model error}} + \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}[\hat{f}]}_{\text{estimation noise}} + \sigma^2_\varepsilon.$$

This result is the central diagnostic of data mining: increasing model complexity reduces bias but inflates variance, and the irreducible noise $\sigma^2_\varepsilon$ sets a hard floor on achievable accuracy.

Lab cell.

import numpy as np
from sympy import symbols, Matrix, simplify

lam = 0.1
X = np.random.randn(100, 2)
y = X @ np.array([0.42, 1.17]) + 0.05 * np.random.randn(100)
w_hat = np.linalg.solve(X.T @ X + lam * np.eye(2), X.T @ y)
print("Estimated weights:", w_hat)

Every concept developed in this book — polynomial bases, eigendecomposition, gradient descent, probability distributions — converges here. The invitation now is to choose one domain and follow it to its frontier: take the ridge regression above, replace the academic features with electronic health records, and you stand at the threshold of clinical risk prediction.
