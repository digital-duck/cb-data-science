Information Entropy

Definition. Let $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ be a probability space and $X$ a discrete random variable taking values in a finite alphabet $\mathcal{X} = \{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}$ with distribution $p(x) = \Pr[X = x]$. The Shannon entropy of $X$ is

$$H(X) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} p(x) \log_2 p(x),$$

with the convention $0 \log 0 = 0$. Units are bits when the logarithm is base-2, nats for base-$e$. The quantity $H$ is not an intrinsic property of $\mathcal{X}$ but of the measure $p$, a distinction that becomes load-bearing in the continuous generalisation, where the differential entropy $h(X) = -\int p(x)\log p(x)\,dx$ loses non-negativity and invariance under coordinate change.

Theorem (Shannon, 1948). Among all probability distributions on an alphabet of size $n$, the uniform distribution $p(x) = 1/n$ uniquely maximises $H$, achieving $H = \log_2 n$ bits. Moreover, $H$ is the unique functional (up to a positive constant) satisfying: (i) continuity in $p$, (ii) symmetry in the arguments, and (iii) the chain rule $H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y\mid X)$.

Proof sketch. Concavity of $-t\log t$ and Jensen's inequality give $H(p) \leq \log n$ with equality iff $p$ is uniform. Uniqueness via the axiomatic characterisation follows by showing any functional satisfying (i)–(iii) must agree with $H$ on rational probabilities and, by continuity, on all of $[0,1]^n$; see Khinchin (1957) for the complete argument. $\square$

Literature context. Shannon's 1948 paper A Mathematical Theory of Communication (Bell Syst. Tech. J.) established $H$ simultaneously as a measure of uncertainty, a lower bound on lossless compression, and—via the source coding theorem—an operational limit: no prefix-free code achieves expected length below $H(X)$ bits per symbol, and Huffman coding reaches $H(X) + 1$. The connection to statistical mechanics runs deep: Jaynes (1957, Phys. Rev.) showed that maximising entropy subject to moment constraints recovers the canonical ensemble, reframing thermodynamic equilibrium as an inference problem. What remains genuinely open is the right entropy for quantum and non-extensive systems: Tsallis entropy $S_q = (1 - \sum p_i^q)/(q-1)$ and Rényi entropy $H_\alpha = \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\sum p_i^\alpha$ each generalise Shannon's functional and collapse to it as $q,\alpha \to 1$, yet their operational coding theorems are not as tight, and their relationships to physical entropy in non-equilibrium settings remain active research (see Wilde, Quantum Information Theory, 2nd ed., 2017, Ch. 10–11).

Suggested investigation. A natural entry project is empirical verification of the source coding theorem under a controlled generative model. Fix a Markov chain on $\{A, C, G, T\}$ with known transition matrix $\mathbf{P}$; the entropy rate is $\bar{H} = -\sum_{i,j}\pi_i P_{ij}\log P_{ij}$ where $\pi$ is the stationary distribution. Generate $10^4$–$10^6$ symbol sequences, apply Huffman and arithmetic coding, and compare achieved compression rates against $\bar{H}$. A meaningful extension: vary $\mathbf{P}$ to interpolate between uniform (maximum entropy, incompressible) and deterministic (zero entropy, trivially compressed), and fit the empirical compression gap as a function of spectral gap $1 - \lambda_2(\mathbf{P})$. The convergence rate to the entropy rate is controlled by the mixing time, connecting to Markov chain theory in a way the standard coding-theory treatment elides.

Report-writing note. A write-up of this investigation should: state the entropy rate formula and derive it from the ergodic theorem for Markov chains; report both the theoretical $\bar{H}$ and empirical code lengths with confidence intervals across realisations; include a log-log plot of compression gap versus sequence length to characterise finite-sample convergence; and discuss where the arithmetic coder outperforms Huffman and why (arithmetic coding achieves $\bar{H}$ exactly in the limit, while Huffman has an integer-length constraint). Situate findings relative to the asymptotic equipartition property (AEP), which should be stated and verified empirically as a secondary result.
