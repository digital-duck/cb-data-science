Bootstrapping

Imagine you want to make a campfire, but you have no matches. All you have is two sticks. So you rub them together to make a spark, use the spark to light some dry grass, and use the grass to light bigger sticks. You built a fire using only things you already had.

That is bootstrapping: starting something up using only yourself.

The funny name comes from an old story about a man who was stuck in a swamp. He grabbed the straps on the back of his boots — called bootstraps — and pulled himself out. It sounds impossible, but the idea stuck: lifting yourself up with your own effort.

Computers do this every time you turn them on. The computer starts almost completely "asleep." A tiny set of instructions wakes it up just enough to load slightly bigger instructions, which load even bigger ones, until the whole system is running. Each step pulls up the next step.

The key idea is a chain of small steps where each step makes the next step possible — starting from almost nothing.

Now you try: Can you think of something in your life that started very small but grew by using itself? A plant growing from a tiny seed counts!
