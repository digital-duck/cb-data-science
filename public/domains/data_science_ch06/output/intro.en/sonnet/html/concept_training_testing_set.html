The Pancake Practice Problem

Imagine you want to teach your little sister to flip pancakes. You show her the same three pancakes over and over until she gets them perfect. Then one morning, a guest arrives and you hand her a brand-new recipe — strawberry pancakes she's never seen before. Can she do it?

That's exactly the problem computers face when they learn.

The Big Idea

When we teach a computer to recognize pictures of cats, we show it thousands of examples. But if we only test it on the same pictures it studied, we're cheating — like letting a student read the test answers beforehand. The grade looks great, but nothing was really learned.

So we split our examples into two piles:

  • Training set — the pancakes your sister practices on
  • Testing set — the surprise strawberry pancakes she's never flipped before

    • The testing set stays hidden until the very end. Only then do we see if the computer truly learned — or just memorized.
