Probability Distribution

You already know that probability measures how likely a single outcome is. A probability distribution takes that idea further: instead of asking "what's the chance of rolling a 3?", it asks "what does the entire picture of chances look like for every possible outcome?"

Think of it as a full report card, not just one grade.

Exploring the structure. For a fair six-sided die, every face has probability $\frac{1}{6}$. List all outcomes and their probabilities together, and you have a distribution:

$$P(X = k) = \frac{1}{6}, \quad k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$$

Here $X$ is called a random variable — a variable whose value depends on chance. The distribution tells you $P(X = k)$ for every possible $k$.

The golden rule. No matter what the distribution looks like, all probabilities must be non-negative and must sum to exactly 1:

$$\sum_{\text{all } k} P(X = k) = 1$$

This makes sense: something has to happen, and the total chance of all possibilities together is certainty.

Distributions don't have to be uniform. A loaded die might give $P(X = 6) = \frac{1}{2}$ and split the remaining $\frac{1}{2}$ across the other five faces. As long as everything adds to 1, it's a valid distribution.

Practice problem. A spinner has three sections: red, blue, and green. You're told $P(\text{red}) = 0.4$ and $P(\text{blue}) = 0.35$.

  • What must $P(\text{green})$ equal? Explain your reasoning using the golden rule.
