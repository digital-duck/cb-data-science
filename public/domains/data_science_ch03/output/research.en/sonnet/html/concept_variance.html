Variance

Definition. Let $X$ be a random variable with finite mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]$. The variance of $X$ is defined as

$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[(X - \mu)^2\right].$$

Variance measures the average squared deviation of $X$ from its mean. Because the deviation is squared before averaging, large departures from $\mu$ are penalized disproportionately. The standard deviation $\sigma = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}$ restores the original units. For a discrete variable taking values $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ with probabilities $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n$, the formula becomes

$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i\,(x_i - \mu)^2.$$

Worked Example. Suppose a fair six-sided die is rolled once. The sample space is $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$, each outcome occurring with probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$. The mean is

$$\mu = \frac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5.$$

The variance is then

$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=1}^{6}(k - 3.5)^2 = \frac{1}{6}\bigl(6.25 + 2.25 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 2.25 + 6.25\bigr) = \frac{17.5}{6} \approx 2.917.$$

The standard deviation is $\sigma \approx 1.708$, meaning a typical roll deviates from the mean by roughly 1.7 units.

Key Theorem (Computational Formula). For any random variable $X$ with finite second moment,

$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - \left(\mathbb{E}[X]\right)^2.$$

Proof sketch. Expand $(X - \mu)^2 = X^2 - 2\mu X + \mu^2$ and apply linearity of expectation:

$$\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^2] = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - 2\mu\,\mathbb{E}[X] + \mu^2 = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - \mu^2. \qquad \square$$

This identity is computationally convenient because $\mathbb{E}[X^2]$ and $\mathbb{E}[X]$ can be computed in a single pass over the data.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Rational, sqrt, symbols, summation, simplify

k = symbols('k', integer=True)
outcomes = [Rational(i) for i in range(1, 7)]
mu = sum(outcomes) / 6
var = sum((x - mu)**2 for x in outcomes) / 6
print("Mean:", mu)           # 7/2
print("Variance:", var)      # 35/12
print("Std dev:", sqrt(var)) # sqrt(105)/6
