Concept Book: Executive Dashboard

Data Science Ch10 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

You have spent this entire journey learning to collect, organize, and interpret information — and now you arrive at the place where all of that skill becomes useful to someone who has to act. An executive dashboard is the art of distilling many moving numbers into a single, honest picture so that a decision-maker can see the story at a glance.

Think back to every concept that led here: ratios told you what to compare; percentages turned raw counts into fair shares; averages smoothed out noise; trend lines captured direction. The dashboard does not replace any of those tools — it assembles them. It asks: which few metrics, shown together, reveal whether things are going well or going wrong?

The central design principle is signal over noise. If you have $n$ possible metrics, a good dashboard shows only the $k \ll n$ that change the decision. A useful rule of thumb:

$$\text{Dashboard KPIs} \leq \sqrt{n}$$

That is not a law — it is a discipline. More panels do not mean more clarity; they often mean less.

Because the dashboard synthesizes rather than specializes, it becomes the natural endpoint of almost any quantitative journey. A hospital administrator uses it to monitor patient wait times and bed capacity simultaneously. A school principal tracks attendance, grades, and disciplinary trends side by side. A small business owner watches revenue, expenses, and customer returns on one screen before every morning meeting.

Practice problem: You run the school store and track three things each week: units sold, total revenue, and number of complaints. Design a mini-dashboard: pick one number from each category that best summarizes the week, and explain in one sentence why you chose it over the alternatives.
